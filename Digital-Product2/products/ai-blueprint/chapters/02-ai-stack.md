# Chapter 2: Your AI Stack

## The Tools Every AI-Powered Founder Needs

You do not need twenty tools. You need the right four or five, configured properly, and running consistently. Here is what actually matters.

### 1. A Core Language Model (Your Brain)

Everything starts with a large language model. This is what does the thinking: writing content, analyzing data, brainstorming strategy, and making decisions.

Your options are Claude, GPT-4, Gemini, and a handful of open-source models. For most founders, Claude or GPT-4 are the right choice. They are the most capable, the most reliable, and the easiest to integrate with other tools.

Pick one. Learn it well. You can always add a second later. Do not spend three weeks comparing benchmarks. The differences between the top models are marginal for business use cases. The difference between using one consistently and not using one at all is enormous.

### 2. A Coding Assistant (Your Builder)

Even if you are not a developer, coding assistants let you build tools, automations, and simple applications without hiring anyone. Cursor, Claude Code, and GitHub Copilot can generate scripts, build dashboards, create APIs, and debug issues in minutes.

If you are technical, these tools make you 5-10x faster. If you are not technical, they make previously impossible things possible. Either way, they pay for themselves on day one.

### 3. An Automation Platform (Your Connector)

Automation platforms are the glue between your tools. They let you say things like "when a new email arrives, extract the key details, score the lead, and add it to my CRM." The major options are Make (formerly Integromat), Zapier, and n8n.

Make and n8n give you the most flexibility. Zapier is the simplest to start with. n8n is free and self-hosted if you want full control.

### 4. An Agent Framework (Your Workforce)

Agent frameworks let you deploy autonomous AI agents that run on schedules, maintain memory between sessions, and execute multi-step tasks. This is where the real leverage lives. CrewAI, AutoGen, LangGraph, and several other frameworks let you build agents that operate independently.

You do not need to master these on day one. Start with simple automations, then graduate to agents when you are ready for autonomous workflows.

### 5. Communication Tools (Your Delivery Layer)

Your agents need a way to reach you. Telegram bots, Slack integrations, email automation, and webhook notifications are the most common delivery channels. Telegram is particularly good for solo founders because it is free, fast, and supports rich formatting.

## How to Start Without Overthinking It

Here is the move: sign up for one LLM (Claude or GPT), install one coding assistant, and set up one automation. Do not try to build the full stack on day one.

Your first week should look like this:

1. **Day 1-2**: Get comfortable with your LLM. Use it for everything: emails, research, planning, content drafts.
2. **Day 3-4**: Set up your coding assistant. Build something small: a script that formats data, a simple web scraper, or a report generator.
3. **Day 5-7**: Connect your first automation. Pick one repetitive task you do every week and automate it.

The stack grows as your needs grow. The founders who win are not the ones with the fanciest setup. They are the ones who actually use what they have, consistently, every single day.
