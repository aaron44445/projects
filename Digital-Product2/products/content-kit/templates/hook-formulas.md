# Hook Formula Guide

20 proven hook formulas with fill-in-the-blank structure, real examples, the psychology behind why they work, and which platforms and content types they're best for.

---

## 1. "Stop doing [X]. Do [Y] instead."

**The formula:**
"Stop [COMMON ACTION/APPROACH]. [DO THIS BETTER ALTERNATIVE] instead."

**Example:**
"Stop adding features to your product. Start talking to your customers instead."

**Why it works:** Pattern interruption. People are drawn to being told they're doing something wrong because it triggers loss aversion — the fear of missing out on a better approach.

**Best for:** Twitter single tweets, TikTok hooks, LinkedIn posts. Works best when the contrarian advice is genuinely useful, not just provocative.

---

## 2. "I spent [time] learning [skill] so you don't have to."

**The formula:**
"I spent [SPECIFIC TIMEFRAME] [LEARNING/RESEARCHING/TESTING] [TOPIC] so you don't have to."

**Example:**
"I spent 6 months learning email marketing so you don't have to. Here are the only 3 things that actually matter."

**Why it works:** It offers a shortcut. People value condensed knowledge because it saves them time and effort. The specific timeframe adds credibility and signals real investment.

**Best for:** Twitter threads, TikTok videos, YouTube. Great as a thread opener or video hook where you then deliver the condensed lessons.

---

## 3. "[Number] [things] that [result]"

**The formula:**
"[SPECIFIC NUMBER] [THINGS/TOOLS/HABITS/MISTAKES] that [POSITIVE OR NEGATIVE RESULT]."

**Example:**
"7 free tools that replaced my entire $200/month tech stack."

**Why it works:** Numbers create specificity and set expectations. The reader knows exactly what they're getting. Odd numbers (3, 5, 7) tend to outperform even numbers because they feel less manufactured.

**Best for:** All platforms. Carousels, threads, listicle videos. This is the most versatile hook formula — works everywhere.

---

## 4. "Nobody talks about this but..."

**The formula:**
"Nobody talks about this, but [INSIDER KNOWLEDGE OR UNCOMFORTABLE TRUTH]."

**Example:**
"Nobody talks about this, but most 'passive income' products take 200+ hours to create."

**Why it works:** Creates a sense of exclusivity and insider access. The reader feels like they're about to learn something hidden from the mainstream, which triggers curiosity and a desire to feel "in the know."

**Best for:** Twitter single tweets, TikTok hooks, Instagram captions. Particularly powerful when the reveal genuinely surprises people.

---

## 5. "The [adjective] way to [achieve goal]"

**The formula:**
"The [FASTEST/EASIEST/LAZIEST/SIMPLEST/CHEAPEST] way to [DESIRABLE OUTCOME]."

**Example:**
"The laziest way to get your first 100 email subscribers."

**Why it works:** Adjectives like "laziest" or "simplest" lower the perceived effort barrier. People want results with minimum effort, and this hook promises exactly that. It reframes a hard goal as achievable.

**Best for:** TikTok videos, Twitter threads, blog post titles. Works best when you actually deliver a genuinely simple method.

---

## 6. "I wish I knew this before I [action]"

**The formula:**
"I wish I knew this before I [STARTED SPECIFIC ACTION/JOURNEY]."

**Example:**
"I wish I knew this before I launched my first digital product."

**Why it works:** It implies hard-won experience and triggers the reader's desire to avoid making the same mistakes. People want to skip the pain of learning things the hard way.

**Best for:** TikTok videos, carousel cover slides, Twitter threads. Great for reflection and lesson-sharing content.

---

## 7. "This is why you're not [achieving result]"

**The formula:**
"This is why you're not [DESIRABLE OUTCOME] (and what to do about it)."

**Example:**
"This is why you're not making money from your side project."

**Why it works:** It directly addresses the reader's frustration and implies you have the answer. The "why" element is powerful because people stuck on a problem desperately want to understand the root cause.

**Best for:** TikTok hooks, Twitter single tweets, Instagram Reels. Works best with a genuine, non-obvious insight — not just "you're not working hard enough."

---

## 8. "How I [achieved result] in [timeframe] (step by step)"

**The formula:**
"How I [SPECIFIC ACHIEVEMENT] in [SPECIFIC TIMEFRAME] (step by step)."

**Example:**
"How I made $2,400 from a digital product in 30 days (step by step)."

**Why it works:** Combines social proof (you did it) with a clear framework (step by step). The specific numbers and timeframe make it believable and the "step by step" promises actionable content, not vague inspiration.

**Best for:** Twitter threads, YouTube videos, TikTok tutorials. The stronger the result and shorter the timeframe, the more compelling the hook.

---

## 9. "POV: [scenario]"

**The formula:**
"POV: [RELATABLE OR ASPIRATIONAL SCENARIO]."

**Example:**
"POV: You just got your first notification that someone bought your digital product while you were sleeping."

**Why it works:** POV hooks create an immersive mental image. The viewer/reader projects themselves into the scenario, which creates an emotional connection before the content even starts.

**Best for:** TikTok (native POV format), Instagram Reels, short-form video. Works best when the scenario is specific and emotionally charged — either aspirational or deeply relatable.

---

## 10. "You need to stop [common mistake] right now"

**The formula:**
"You need to stop [COMMON MISTAKE/BAD HABIT] right now. Here's why."

**Example:**
"You need to stop building products nobody asked for. Right now."

**Why it works:** Urgency ("right now") plus direct address ("you") creates a personal call to action. It implies the reader is actively losing something by continuing their current behavior.

**Best for:** TikTok hooks, Twitter tweets, Instagram captions. The urgency should feel genuine, not clickbaity. Follow through with a real explanation.

---

## 11. "The secret to [result] that [experts] won't tell you"

**The formula:**
"The secret to [DESIRABLE RESULT] that [AUTHORITY FIGURES/GURUS/EXPERTS] won't tell you."

**Example:**
"The secret to growing on Twitter that marketing gurus won't tell you: reply to other people's posts more than you post your own."

**Why it works:** It positions you as a truth-teller going against the establishment. People distrust polished advice from "experts" and crave authentic, contrarian insights from real practitioners.

**Best for:** TikTok hooks, Twitter single tweets, LinkedIn posts. Works best when your "secret" is genuinely contrarian and backed by real experience.

---

## 12. "[Famous person] does [thing]. Here's why it works."

**The formula:**
"[KNOWN PERSON/COMPANY] [DOES SPECIFIC THING]. Here's why it works."

**Example:**
"Pieter Levels builds products with just HTML and JavaScript. Here's why it works better than your React app."

**Why it works:** Name recognition stops the scroll. Associating your insight with a known figure borrows their credibility and makes the reader curious about what specific behavior you've identified.

**Best for:** Twitter threads, LinkedIn posts, TikTok. Most effective when you add your own original analysis rather than just praising the person.

---

## 13. "I tried [X] for [time]. Here are the results."

**The formula:**
"I tried [SPECIFIC THING] for [SPECIFIC TIMEFRAME]. Here are the results."

**Example:**
"I tried posting on TikTok every day for 60 days. Here are the results."

**Why it works:** It's a personal experiment with a defined scope. People love seeing results from someone who actually tested something — it saves them the time and risk of trying it themselves.

**Best for:** All platforms. Threads, videos, carousels. The longer the timeframe and the more specific the results, the more compelling the hook.

---

## 14. "Warning: This will change how you think about [topic]"

**The formula:**
"Warning: This will change how you think about [TOPIC/CONCEPT]."

**Example:**
"Warning: This will change how you think about pricing your products."

**Why it works:** The "warning" creates a micro-commitment — the reader is now braced for a paradigm shift, which makes them more likely to engage deeply with what follows. It promises transformation, not just information.

**Best for:** Twitter thread openers, TikTok hooks, email subject lines. Only use this when you genuinely have a perspective-shifting insight.

---

## 15. "Unpopular opinion: [controversial take]"

**The formula:**
"Unpopular opinion: [YOUR GENUINE CONTRARIAN BELIEF ABOUT YOUR NICHE]."

**Example:**
"Unpopular opinion: You don't need a logo, a brand guide, or a domain name to make your first $1,000 online."

**Why it works:** People are wired to react to opinions they disagree with. This hook generates engagement because both supporters and opponents feel compelled to respond. Disagreement is the strongest engagement driver on social media.

**Best for:** Twitter single tweets, TikTok hooks, LinkedIn posts. Be prepared for debate. Works best when your opinion is defensible and comes from real experience.

---

## 16. "If you're [situation], watch this."

**The formula:**
"If you're [SPECIFIC SITUATION YOUR AUDIENCE IS IN], [WATCH THIS / READ THIS / LISTEN UP]."

**Example:**
"If you're stuck trying to get your first 10 customers, watch this."

**Why it works:** It pre-qualifies the audience. The reader self-selects — if they're in that situation, they feel the content was made specifically for them. This personal relevance is more powerful than broad appeals.

**Best for:** TikTok hooks, Instagram Reels, YouTube thumbnails/titles. The more specific the situation, the stronger the hook. "If you're a developer trying to sell your first SaaS" beats "If you want to make money online."

---

## 17. "The [number]-step system for [result]"

**The formula:**
"The [NUMBER]-step system for [SPECIFIC RESULT]."

**Example:**
"The 4-step system for going from idea to launched product in one weekend."

**Why it works:** "System" implies a repeatable, reliable process — not a one-time hack. People want systems because they promise predictable outcomes. The specific number makes it feel structured and achievable.

**Best for:** Twitter threads, carousels, YouTube videos. This hook works best as a content framework where each step gets its own tweet, slide, or segment.

---

## 18. "[Result] isn't about [common belief]. It's about [truth]."

**The formula:**
"[DESIRABLE RESULT] isn't about [COMMON ASSUMPTION]. It's about [YOUR INSIGHT]."

**Example:**
"Making money online isn't about finding the right niche. It's about solving one problem really well."

**Why it works:** It corrects a misconception, which makes the reader reassess their own approach. The structure creates a clean contrast between the wrong way and the right way of thinking.

**Best for:** Twitter single tweets, LinkedIn posts, carousel cover slides. Works best when the "common belief" is something your audience genuinely holds.

---

## 19. "What [time period] of [activity] taught me about [bigger lesson]"

**The formula:**
"What [SPECIFIC TIMEFRAME] of [SPECIFIC ACTIVITY] taught me about [BROADER CONCEPT]."

**Example:**
"What 2 years of building in public taught me about trust."

**Why it works:** It takes a specific personal experience and extracts a universal lesson. The contrast between the concrete (timeframe + activity) and the abstract (bigger lesson) creates intrigue about what the connection is.

**Best for:** Twitter threads, LinkedIn long-form posts, TikTok storytelling. This is excellent for reflection content and building authority through experience.

---

## 20. "Most people [common approach]. Top [percentage] do this instead."

**The formula:**
"Most people [COMMON/DEFAULT BEHAVIOR]. The top [PERCENTAGE] [DO SOMETHING DIFFERENT]."

**Example:**
"Most people launch their product on Monday morning. The top 1% launch on Tuesday at 10 AM. Here's why."

**Why it works:** It creates an in-group/out-group dynamic. Nobody wants to be "most people" — everyone wants to be in the elite percentage. This hook leverages social comparison to drive engagement.

**Best for:** TikTok hooks, Twitter tweets, Instagram captions. The key is that the "top percentage" behavior should be genuinely different and defensible, not just contrarian for shock value.
