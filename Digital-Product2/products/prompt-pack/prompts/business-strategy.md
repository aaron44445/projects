# Business Strategy Prompts

## 1. Competitive Landscape Scanner

**The Prompt:**

> I'm running [one sentence about your business]. Act as a market intelligence analyst. Map out my competitive landscape: identify my 3 real competitors (not who I think they are — who customers actually choose instead), their core weakness I can exploit, and the one positioning angle that makes me impossible to compare. Be ruthlessly specific. If you need clarification, ask one pointed question.

**When to use it:** Before finalizing your marketing message or when competitor research feels overwhelming.

**Pro tip:** The AI often identifies non-obvious competitors (alternatives, not direct clones) — pay attention to those.

---

## 2. Business Model Stress Test

**The Prompt:**

> I'm building [one sentence about your business]. You're a skeptical investor who's seen 1,000 pitches. Tell me: the single biggest assumption I'm making that's probably wrong, the one cost I'm underestimating, and whether my business has a "rich person problem" or a "real person problem." Don't sugarcoat it. If something's unclear, ask me one clarifying question.

**When to use it:** Before committing serious time or money, or when something feels off but you can't pinpoint it.

**Pro tip:** The "rich vs real problem" distinction often reveals if you have product-market fit or just a feature.

---

## 3. Customer Persona Deep Dive

**The Prompt:**

> My customer is [one sentence description]. Act as a consumer psychologist. Tell me: what they're doing the hour before they need my solution, the exact words they use when complaining about this problem to a friend, and the real reason they haven't solved this yet (not the reason they'll tell me). Be specific enough that I'd recognize them in a coffee shop. Need more context? Ask me one question.

**When to use it:** When your marketing feels generic or when you're guessing at customer motivations.

**Pro tip:** The "words they use with a friend" insight is gold for landing page copy and ad targeting.

---

## 4. Pricing Strategy Optimizer

**The Prompt:**

> I'm selling [one sentence about your offer]. Act as a pricing strategist. Give me three specific price points: the "coward's price" (too low), the "goldilocks price" (probably right), and the "bold price" (tests if I'm undervaluing). For each, tell me the exact customer segment it attracts and why. Then tell me which one to start with and why. Be specific with dollar amounts. If you need more info, ask one question.

**When to use it:** When launching, when revenue feels stuck, or when you're afraid to charge more.

**Pro tip:** Test the "bold price" with your next 10 customers — you'll be surprised how often it works.

---

## 5. SWOT Analysis That Actually Helps

**The Prompt:**

> I'm running [one sentence about your business]. Do a SWOT analysis, but make it actually useful: Strengths (pick ONE to double down on), Weaknesses (pick ONE that's actually killing growth), Opportunities (pick ONE I can act on this month), Threats (pick ONE that could end me in 12 months). For each, give me a specific action, not a vague observation. If you need context, ask one question.

**When to use it:** Quarterly planning, when you feel scattered, or when "everything matters" and you can't prioritize.

**Pro tip:** The ONE weakness is usually what you're avoiding — start there.

---

## 6. Revenue Forecasting Framework

**The Prompt:**

> I'm at [current monthly revenue or "pre-revenue"]. My business is [one sentence]. Act as a CFO. Build me a 12-month revenue forecast with three scenarios: pessimistic (if things go badly), realistic (if I execute decently), optimistic (if I nail it). For each month, tell me the key assumption that makes or breaks the forecast. Use actual numbers. If you need more info, ask one question at a time.

**When to use it:** Planning runway, pitching investors, or deciding if you can quit your job.

**Pro tip:** The pessimistic scenario is your real plan — if you can't stomach it, don't start.

---

## 7. Partnership Evaluation Matrix

**The Prompt:**

> I'm considering partnering with [type of partner or specific company]. My business is [one sentence]. Act as a deal advisor. Score this partnership 0-10 on: strategic fit, revenue potential, operational headache, and brand risk. Then tell me the ONE question I should ask them to know if this is real, the ONE thing I should negotiate hard on, and whether to do this deal. Be direct. Need more context? Ask me one question.

**When to use it:** Before saying yes to any partnership, collaboration, or joint venture.

**Pro tip:** If the "brand risk" score is above 6, walk away — no amount of revenue is worth it.

## 8. MVP Scope Definer

**The Prompt:**

> I'm building [one sentence about your product]. Act as a ruthless product manager. Tell me: the ONE core feature that must work perfectly, the 3 features I think I need but should cut, and the "magic moment" when a user goes from skeptical to hooked. Then give me a 4-week build plan with weekly milestones. Be brutally minimal. If you need clarification, ask one question.

**When to use it:** Before building anything, when scope is creeping, or when your MVP has 47 features.

**Pro tip:** If you can't describe the "magic moment" in one sentence, your MVP isn't focused enough.

---

## 9. Product Positioning Statement Builder

**The Prompt:**

> I'm selling [one sentence about your product]. Act as a brand strategist. Write me a positioning statement: "For [specific who], who [specific problem], [my product] is a [category] that [unique benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [key differentiator]." Make it so specific that it excludes 80% of people — that's the point. Then tell me the one tagline to test. Need more info? Ask one question.

**When to use it:** When your messaging feels generic, before a website rewrite, or when nobody understands what you do.

**Pro tip:** If your positioning could apply to 3+ competitors, it's too broad — start over.

---

## 10. Market Entry Risk Assessment

**The Prompt:**

> I'm entering [market/industry] with [one sentence about my business]. Act as a risk analyst. Identify: the regulatory landmine I'm probably ignoring, the incumbent who will crush me if I get on their radar, the customer expectation I can't afford to meet, and the one "unfair advantage" I need to survive. Be specific and scary. If something's unclear, ask me one targeted question.

**When to use it:** Before entering a new market, pivoting, or expanding to a new customer segment.

**Pro tip:** The "unfair advantage" requirement is your filter — if you don't have one, pick a different market.

---

## 11. Customer Retention Strategy

**The Prompt:**

> I'm running [one sentence about your business]. My customers [describe churn situation or "are new"]. Act as a retention expert. Tell me: the exact moment when customers mentally check out (be specific — "day 3" not "early on"), the ONE feature or touchpoint that would make them stay, and a 30-day re-engagement plan for at-risk customers. Give me specific tactics, not theory. Need more context? Ask one question.

**When to use it:** When churn feels high, when growth is masking retention problems, or before investing in acquisition.

**Pro tip:** Retention improvements compound — a 5% boost beats a 20% acquisition increase over 12 months.

---

## 12. Investor Pitch Deck Outliner

**The Prompt:**

> I'm raising [amount or "pre-seed"] for [one sentence about my business]. Act as a pitch coach who's helped 100+ founders raise. Outline my 10-slide deck: tell me the exact story arc, the ONE slide that will make or break the pitch, the objection I need to preempt (and on which slide), and the three numbers that must be on the traction slide. Be opinionated about structure. If you need more info, ask one question.

**When to use it:** Before building a deck, when investor meetings aren't converting, or when your pitch feels flat.

**Pro tip:** The slide after your "big idea" slide is where investors decide — make it your strongest proof point.

---

## How to Use These Prompts

1. **Copy the entire prompt** (including the role instruction)
2. **Fill in the [bracket]** with one concise sentence
3. **Paste into ChatGPT or Claude** and hit enter
4. **Let the AI ask follow-up questions** if it needs more context (this is faster than front-loading everything)
5. **Push back if the answer is generic** — say "be more specific" or "give me actual numbers"

These prompts are designed to give you strategic clarity in under 5 minutes. No MBA required.
