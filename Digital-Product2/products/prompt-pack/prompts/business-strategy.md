# Business Strategy Prompts

## 1. Competitive Landscape Scanner

**The Prompt:**

> I'm building [describe your product/service] targeting [audience]. Identify my top 5 likely competitors. For each one, give me:
>
> 1. Their core value proposition (one sentence)
> 2. Their pricing model and approximate price point
> 3. Their biggest strength I should respect
> 4. Their biggest weakness I can exploit
> 5. What their customers complain about most (based on likely review patterns)
>
> Then tell me: what gap exists in this market that none of them are filling?

**When to use it:** Before entering a new market or launching a product, to understand who you're up against and where the opening is.

**Pro tip:** Feed in a specific niche rather than a broad category — "AI-powered meal planning for bodybuilders" beats "fitness apps" every time.

## 2. Business Model Stress Test

**The Prompt:**

> Here's my business model: [describe how you make money, your costs, your customer acquisition approach]. Play the role of a brutally honest startup advisor who has seen 500 businesses fail. Poke holes in this model by answering:
>
> 1. What are the 3 most likely reasons this fails in the first year?
> 2. What hidden costs am I probably not accounting for?
> 3. What happens to my unit economics if customer acquisition cost doubles?
> 4. Is this a venture-scale business, a lifestyle business, or a side hustle? Be honest.
> 5. What's the one metric I should obsess over in the first 90 days?
>
> Don't sugarcoat anything. I need the truth, not encouragement.

**When to use it:** After you've drafted your business plan but before you invest serious money or time.

**Pro tip:** Run this prompt 3 times and compare answers — the issues that come up every time are the real threats.

## 3. Customer Persona Deep Dive

**The Prompt:**

> I sell [product/service] to [general audience]. Build me a detailed customer persona by answering every question below:
>
> **Demographics:** Age range, income level, job title, location, education
> **Psychographics:** What keeps them up at night? What do they secretly want but won't say out loud? What do they brag about to friends?
> **Buying behavior:** Where do they discover new products? What makes them pull the trigger on a purchase? What makes them abandon a cart?
> **Day in their life:** Walk me through a typical Tuesday for this person, from waking up to going to bed. Where does my product fit into this day?
> **The trigger moment:** What specific event or frustration makes them Google a solution like mine?
>
> Give this persona a name, age, and one-sentence backstory.

**When to use it:** When creating marketing materials, designing features, or deciding what to build next.

**Pro tip:** Create 2-3 personas and then ask the AI "which of these three would pay the most and churn the least?" to find your ideal customer.

## 4. Pricing Strategy Optimizer

**The Prompt:**

> I'm pricing [product/service]. Here's what I know:
> - My cost to deliver: [amount]
> - Competitor prices: [range]
> - My target customer: [description]
> - My key differentiator: [what makes me different]
>
> Give me 3 different pricing strategies I could use, with the specific price point for each:
>
> 1. **Penetration pricing** — optimized to grab market share fast
> 2. **Value-based pricing** — optimized for maximum revenue per customer
> 3. **Tiered pricing** — a good/better/best structure with specific features at each tier
>
> For each strategy, tell me: what type of customer it attracts, the biggest risk, and what signal would tell me to raise or lower the price. End with your recommendation and why.

**When to use it:** When launching a new product or feeling like your current pricing is leaving money on the table.

**Pro tip:** Add "My customers currently pay [X] for [alternative solution]" to anchor the AI's suggestions in market reality.

## 5. SWOT Analysis That Actually Helps

**The Prompt:**

> Run a SWOT analysis on my business, but make it actionable — not just a list of words in boxes.
>
> My business: [describe what you do, how you make money, your current stage]
>
> For each quadrant, give me:
> - **Strengths:** 3 strengths, and for each one, a specific way I should be leveraging it harder
> - **Weaknesses:** 3 weaknesses, and for each one, a concrete step to mitigate it this month
> - **Opportunities:** 3 opportunities, ranked by effort-to-impact ratio, with a first step for each
> - **Threats:** 3 threats, ranked by likelihood, with an early warning sign I should watch for
>
> Then give me a one-paragraph "strategic summary" — if you could only give me one piece of advice based on this analysis, what would it be?

**When to use it:** During quarterly planning or when you feel stuck and need to see your business from 30,000 feet.

**Pro tip:** Do this analysis once per quarter and compare results — the trends in how your SWOT changes over time are more valuable than any single snapshot.

## 6. Revenue Forecasting Framework

**The Prompt:**

> Help me build a 12-month revenue forecast. Here are my inputs:
>
> - Current monthly revenue: [amount]
> - Revenue model: [subscription/one-time/usage-based/etc.]
> - Average deal size: [amount]
> - Current number of customers: [number]
> - Monthly churn rate: [percentage or "I don't know"]
> - Monthly new customers: [number or growth rate]
> - Planned changes: [price increases, new products, new channels, etc.]
>
> Build me three scenarios:
> 1. **Conservative** — things stay roughly the same, no lucky breaks
> 2. **Moderate** — planned initiatives work at 50% effectiveness
> 3. **Optimistic** — things go well but not unrealistically
>
> Present each as a month-by-month table. Highlight the month where I break even (if applicable). Call out the single biggest lever that separates the conservative scenario from the optimistic one.

**When to use it:** When planning budgets, hiring, or deciding whether you can afford to make a big investment.

**Pro tip:** Ask the AI to also calculate the "survival months" — how many months of runway you have in each scenario if revenue drops to zero.

## 7. Partnership Evaluation Matrix

**The Prompt:**

> I'm considering a partnership with [company/person/organization]. Here's what I know about them: [describe what they do, their size, what the partnership would look like].
>
> Evaluate this partnership across these dimensions:
>
> 1. **Strategic alignment:** Do our goals conflict or complement? Where might they diverge in 12 months?
> 2. **Power balance:** Who needs whom more? How does this affect negotiation leverage?
> 3. **Revenue impact:** Model the best-case and worst-case financial outcome over 12 months
> 4. **Reputation risk:** What's the worst thing that could happen to my brand through association?
> 5. **Exit cost:** How hard is it to unwind this partnership if it goes wrong?
> 6. **Opportunity cost:** What am I NOT doing by committing resources to this partnership?
>
> Score each dimension 1-10 and give me a total. Then give me your honest recommendation: pursue, negotiate harder, or walk away.

**When to use it:** Before signing any partnership agreement, affiliate deal, or co-marketing arrangement.

**Pro tip:** Also ask "What three things should I insist on in the contract based on this analysis?" to turn the evaluation into action.

## 8. MVP Scope Definer

**The Prompt:**

> I want to build [describe your product idea]. I have [time/budget constraints]. Help me ruthlessly scope an MVP:
>
> 1. List every feature I'm probably imagining. Be comprehensive — include the ones I haven't mentioned but probably want.
> 2. Now categorize each feature:
>    - **Must have for launch** — the product literally doesn't work without this
>    - **Should have within 30 days** — important but can ship without it
>    - **Nice to have in v2** — adds value but is scope creep right now
>    - **Kill it** — feels important but actually isn't for an MVP
> 3. Define the absolute minimum version that would let me charge money and learn whether customers want this.
> 4. Give me a launch checklist of the non-product things I also need (landing page, payment, analytics, etc.)
>
> Be aggressive about cutting scope. My goal is to launch in [timeframe], not to build the perfect product.

**When to use it:** At the start of any new product build, before writing a single line of code or designing a single screen.

**Pro tip:** After getting the MVP scope, ask "A customer just signed up and is disappointed. What's the most likely reason?" to stress-test whether you cut too much.

## 9. Product Positioning Statement Builder

**The Prompt:**

> Help me write a positioning statement for [product/service]. Here's what I know:
>
> - What it does: [description]
> - Who it's for: [target customer]
> - Key competitors: [list 2-3]
> - What makes us different: [differentiators]
>
> Generate these positioning assets:
>
> 1. **Classic positioning statement:** "For [target customer] who [need], [product] is the [category] that [key benefit] unlike [competitor], we [differentiator]."
> 2. **Elevator pitch:** 30-second version I could say to someone at a conference
> 3. **One-liner:** The sentence I put at the top of my website
> 4. **The "so what" test:** Why should a busy person care about this? Answer in one sentence.
> 5. **Internal rallying cry:** A short phrase my team can use to stay focused on what matters
>
> For each one, give me 2 options — one that emphasizes the rational benefit and one that emphasizes the emotional benefit.

**When to use it:** When building a landing page, investor pitch, or any situation where you need to explain what you do quickly and memorably.

**Pro tip:** Test the one-liner by asking "If I showed this sentence to my target customer with zero context, would they immediately understand if this is for them?"

## 10. Market Entry Risk Assessment

**The Prompt:**

> I'm considering entering the [market/industry] with [product/service]. Before I commit, run a pre-mortem analysis:
>
> 1. **It's 12 months from now and this failed.** Give me the 5 most likely reasons why, ranked by probability.
> 2. **Regulatory and legal risks:** What rules, regulations, or legal issues might I not be aware of in this space?
> 3. **Timing analysis:** Is this market too early (customers aren't ready), too late (saturated), or just right? What evidence supports your answer?
> 4. **Capital requirements:** What's the realistic minimum investment to reach product-market fit, not just launch?
> 5. **First-mover vs. fast-follower:** In this specific market, is it better to be first or to let others validate the market?
>
> End with a go/no-go recommendation and the single most important thing I should validate before committing.

**When to use it:** Before making any significant financial or time commitment to a new market.

**Pro tip:** If the AI says "go," immediately follow up with "You're being too optimistic. What did you gloss over?" to pressure-test the recommendation.

## 11. Customer Retention Strategy

**The Prompt:**

> My business has a churn problem. Here's my situation:
>
> - Product/service: [description]
> - Monthly churn rate: [percentage]
> - Average customer lifetime: [months]
> - Main reasons customers leave: [what you know or suspect]
> - Current retention efforts: [what you're already doing]
>
> Build me a retention strategy:
>
> 1. **Quick wins (this week):** 3 things I can implement immediately that typically reduce churn
> 2. **30-day improvements:** Changes to onboarding, communication, or product that address root causes
> 3. **Systemic fixes:** Longer-term changes to my business model or product that create structural retention
> 4. **The "save" playbook:** Exactly what to say/do when a customer tries to cancel, with 3 different scripts based on their reason for leaving
> 5. **Metrics to track:** Which 3 leading indicators predict churn before it happens?
>
> For each recommendation, estimate the potential impact on churn rate.

**When to use it:** When your churn rate is higher than you'd like, or when you're transitioning from growth-focused to retention-focused.

**Pro tip:** Add your actual cancellation survey responses (anonymized) to give the AI real data to work with instead of generic advice.

## 12. Investor Pitch Deck Outliner

**The Prompt:**

> Help me structure a pitch deck for [company/product]. Here's the raw information:
>
> - Problem we solve: [description]
> - Our solution: [description]
> - Current traction: [revenue, users, growth rate]
> - Business model: [how we make money]
> - Market size: [what you know]
> - Team: [key people and their relevant experience]
> - Ask: [how much funding, what it's for]
>
> Create a 12-slide pitch deck outline. For each slide, give me:
> 1. The slide title
> 2. The one key message this slide must communicate
> 3. The data point or story that makes this slide compelling
> 4. What a skeptical investor would think — and how the next slide answers their objection
>
> Then give me the 3 hardest questions an investor will ask after this presentation, with suggested answers.

**When to use it:** When preparing to raise funding, pitch to accelerators, or present to potential strategic partners.

**Pro tip:** After generating the outline, ask "Rewrite slide 1 assuming the investor has already seen 10 pitches today and is tired" to make your opening impossible to ignore.
