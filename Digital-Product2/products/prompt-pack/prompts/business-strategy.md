# Business Strategy Prompts

## 1. Competitive Landscape Scanner

**The Prompt:**

> You are a market intelligence analyst with 20 years of experience in competitive analysis and strategic positioning.
>
> Analyze the competitive landscape for: [YOUR PRODUCT/SERVICE]
>
> **Industry Context:**
> - Target market: [e.g., B2B SaaS for marketing teams]
> - Price point: [e.g., $49/month]
> - Primary customer pain point: [what problem you solve]
>
> **Deliver:**
>
> 1. **Direct Competitors Matrix** — Identify 5-7 direct competitors and map them across two axes: Price (low to high) and Feature Completeness (basic to advanced). Place each competitor in the grid with a one-sentence positioning statement.
>
> 2. **Competitive Gaps Analysis** — Identify 3 specific underserved segments or feature gaps in the current market that represent genuine opportunities (not just "better customer service").
>
> 3. **Threat Assessment** — For each major competitor, identify their strongest competitive advantage and the specific customer segment where they're unbeatable. Be honest about where you cannot win.
>
> 4. **Positioning Wedge** — Based on the gaps, recommend one specific positioning angle that differentiates you from all competitors. Format: "The only [category] that [unique capability] for [specific audience]."
>
> 5. **Early Warning Signals** — List 5 concrete signals to monitor that would indicate a competitor is about to neutralize your advantage (e.g., job postings, funding announcements, feature releases).
>
> Present this as a strategic brief, not a generic list. Be brutally honest about competitive threats.

**When to use it:** Before finalizing your positioning, pricing, or messaging strategy to ensure you're not walking into a buzzsaw.

**Pro tip:** Include URLs of 2-3 competitor products so the AI can reference real positioning and messaging if it has web access.

## 2. Business Model Stress Test

**The Prompt:**

> You are a business model architect who has advised 200+ startups and seen every failure mode. Your job is to find the breaking points in business models before the market does.
>
> **My Business Model:**
> - What we sell: [product/service]
> - Who pays us: [customer type]
> - Revenue model: [one-time/subscription/usage-based/etc.]
> - Primary cost drivers: [what costs the most to deliver]
> - Current scale: [revenue or customer count]
>
> **Run these 6 stress tests:**
>
> 1. **The Scale Death Spiral** — At what point does growth make the business less profitable instead of more? Identify the specific operational bottleneck that breaks first.
>
> 2. **The Margin Compression Trap** — What market forces or competitor actions could squeeze margins by 50%? How sustainable is current pricing if competitors go aggressive?
>
> 3. **The Customer Concentration Risk** — If we lose our biggest customer or top 20% of customers, what happens to unit economics and fixed costs?
>
> 4. **The Acquisition Cost Inflation** — What happens if CAC doubles in the next 12 months due to market saturation or platform changes? At what CAC does the model break?
>
> 5. **The Churn Cascade** — Model a scenario where churn increases by 5% per month for 6 months. How quickly does the business become unsustainable?
>
> 6. **The Margin-of-Error Test** — If we're wrong about one key assumption (conversion rate, retention, ACV, etc.), which single error would be fatal? Rank assumptions by risk.
>
> **Final Output:** For each breaking point, provide:
> - Early warning metrics to monitor
> - One specific action to reduce that risk NOW
> - The "point of no return" threshold where it's too late to fix
>
> Be the pessimist I need, not the cheerleader I want.

**When to use it:** Before raising money, hiring aggressively, or making any decision that assumes your current business model will scale linearly.

**Pro tip:** Add real numbers (CAC, LTV, churn rate, gross margin) if you have them — the AI will give you quantitative breaking points instead of generic warnings.

## 3. Customer Persona Deep Dive

**The Prompt:**

> You are a customer research expert who has conducted 1000+ customer interviews and built personas for companies like Stripe, Notion, and Figma.
>
> Build a high-resolution persona for my target customer:
>
> **What I Know:**
> - Product: [what you're selling]
> - Basic persona: [e.g., "marketing managers at B2B SaaS companies"]
> - Main pain point: [what problem they have]
>
> **Build a persona that includes:**
>
> 1. **The Day-in-the-Life Scenario** — Describe a typical Tuesday. What do they do from 9am-5pm? What tools do they use? What meetings do they attend? Where does my product fit into their actual workflow?
>
> 2. **The Buying Psychology Profile:**
>    - What does success look like in their role? (How are they measured?)
>    - What are they afraid of? (What gets them fired?)
>    - Who do they need to convince to buy? (Boss, team, procurement?)
>    - What's their budget authority threshold without approval?
>
> 3. **The Objection Hierarchy** — List the 5 objections they'll have in order from first to last. For each, provide the underlying fear and the proof point that dissolves it.
>
> 4. **The Alternatives Framework** — Before they buy from me, what are they doing instead? Be specific: "Using Google Sheets + Slack + Friday meetings" not just "manual process."
>
> 5. **The Language Audit** — What exact phrases do they use to describe their problem? Give me 10 verbatim statements they'd say to a coworker (e.g., "I'm drowning in Slack messages and missing important stuff").
>
> 6. **The Trigger Event Map** — What 5 specific events make them suddenly start looking for a solution like mine? (e.g., "New boss asks for weekly reporting," "Team grows from 3 to 8 people")
>
> 7. **The Content Consumption Profile** — Where do they go to learn? Newsletters they read, podcasts they listen to, Slack/Discord communities they're in, specific subreddits or X accounts they follow.
>
> **Format this as a brief I can give to a copywriter, not a fluffy persona poster.**

**When to use it:** Before writing any marketing copy, building landing pages, or deciding which features to prioritize.

**Pro tip:** If you've done customer interviews, paste 2-3 raw quotes from real customers — the AI will mirror that authentic language throughout the persona.

## 4. Pricing Strategy Optimizer

**The Prompt:**

> You are a pricing strategist who has optimized monetization for 100+ SaaS and digital products, increasing revenue by an average of 34% without losing customers.
>
> **Current Situation:**
> - Product: [what you sell]
> - Current price: [price and model]
> - Target customer: [who buys it]
> - Current conversion rate: [% or "unknown"]
> - Main competitor prices: [list 2-3 competitors and their pricing]
>
> **Analyze and optimize my pricing with this framework:**
>
> 1. **Value Metric Diagnosis** — Am I charging for the right thing? Evaluate whether I should charge by [users/usage/value delivered/flat fee]. For each model, explain which customer segment it attracts and which it repels.
>
> 2. **The Price Anchoring Audit** — Is my pricing structure making my core offer look expensive or cheap? Recommend a 3-tier structure (if I don't have one) with strategic price gaps that make the middle tier obvious.
>
> 3. **The Willingness-to-Pay Ladder** — Map 5 customer segments from lowest to highest willingness to pay. For each segment, identify what additional value would justify 2x the price.
>
> 4. **The Margin Expansion Levers** — Identify 3 ways to increase revenue per customer without adding features:
>    - Packaging changes (bundling, unbundling, tiering)
>    - Add-ons or usage-based components
>    - Implementation, support, or service offerings
>
> 5. **The Grandfathering Strategy** — If I need to raise prices, provide a specific plan: Who gets grandfathered? For how long? What triggers the price increase for existing customers?
>
> 6. **The Discount Danger Audit** — Am I overusing discounts? Recommend which discounts to eliminate and what to offer instead (e.g., extended trials, bonus features, payment plans).
>
> 7. **The Pricing Experiment Roadmap** — Give me 3 specific A/B tests to run in priority order, with hypothesis, success metrics, and statistical significance targets.
>
> **Final Recommendation:** Propose one specific pricing change I should test in the next 30 days with expected impact on MRR and conversion.

**When to use it:** When revenue growth stalls, before a major product launch, or when you suspect you're leaving money on the table.

**Pro tip:** Include your CAC and target LTV:CAC ratio — the AI will ensure pricing recommendations actually support profitable growth, not just higher prices.

## 5. SWOT Analysis That Actually Helps

**The Prompt:**

> You are a strategic advisor who has conducted SWOT analyses for Fortune 500 companies and startups. You know that most SWOT analyses are useless because they're too vague. Your job is to make this one actionable.
>
> **Business Context:**
> - Company/Product: [name and what you do]
> - Stage: [bootstrapped MVP / seed funded / scaling / etc.]
> - Market: [industry and target customer]
> - Goal for next 12 months: [specific objective]
>
> **Conduct a SWOT analysis with these rules:**
>
> **STRENGTHS** — List 4-6 strengths, but for each one:
> - Explain why it's defensible (not easily copied)
> - Identify which customer segment values it most
> - Rate its durability (1-3 years) before competitors neutralize it
>
> **WEAKNESSES** — List 4-6 weaknesses, but for each one:
> - Assess severity: Is this a "deal-killer" or just a "friction point"?
> - Estimate cost/time to fix
> - Decide: Fix it, mitigate it, or accept it?
>
> **OPPORTUNITIES** — List 4-6 opportunities, ranked by:
> - ROI potential (high/medium/low)
> - Time to capture (fast/medium/slow)
> - Probability of success (%)
> - Then recommend: Which ONE should I pursue first and why?
>
> **THREATS** — List 4-6 threats, but for each one:
> - Assign a likelihood (%) and impact (1-10 severity score)
> - Provide an early warning signal to monitor
> - Give one specific mitigation action I can take this month
>
> **Strategic Synthesis:**
> Based on this SWOT, answer:
> 1. What's my single biggest competitive advantage right now?
> 2. What's the one weakness most likely to kill the business if ignored?
> 3. What's the highest-ROI opportunity I should pursue in the next 90 days?
> 4. What's the one threat that keeps you up at night on my behalf?
>
> **Format as a strategic memo, not a 2x2 grid.**

**When to use it:** Quarterly planning, before major strategic pivots, or when you feel stuck and need clarity on priorities.

**Pro tip:** Add a "Recent events" section with 2-3 specific things that happened recently (competitor funding, customer churn spike, feature launch) — the AI will weave these into the analysis.

## 6. Revenue Forecasting Framework

**The Prompt:**

> You are a financial modeler who has built revenue projections for 200+ startups and has seen which assumptions break in reality. Build me a bottoms-up revenue forecast that's actually achievable.
>
> **Current State:**
> - Revenue model: [how you make money]
> - Current MRR/Revenue: [number or "pre-revenue"]
> - Sales cycle length: [days/weeks from lead to close]
> - Current conversion rates: [visitor→lead→customer % or "unknown"]
> - Average deal size: [$ per customer]
> - Current churn rate: [monthly % or "unknown"]
>
> **Time Horizon:** [e.g., next 12 months]
>
> **Build a forecast that includes:**
>
> 1. **The Assumptions Table** — List every assumption I'm making:
>    - Traffic/lead generation rate
>    - Conversion rate at each funnel stage
>    - Average contract value
>    - Churn/retention rate
>    - Expansion revenue rate
>    - For each assumption, mark it: Conservative / Realistic / Optimistic
>
> 2. **The Three-Scenario Model:**
>    - **Pessimistic Case** (30% probability): What if 2-3 key assumptions are wrong? Model it.
>    - **Base Case** (50% probability): Realistic growth with current trajectory
>    - **Optimistic Case** (20% probability): What if things go better than expected?
>
> 3. **The Monthly Build** — Show me month-by-month revenue for the base case, broken down by:
>    - New customer revenue
>    - Expansion revenue (upsells/cross-sells)
>    - Churned revenue (what we lose)
>    - Net new MRR each month
>
> 4. **The Growth Levers Analysis** — For each lever (increase traffic, improve conversion, reduce churn, increase ACV), calculate:
>    - How much would a 10% improvement in this lever increase annual revenue?
>    - Rank levers by ROI potential
>    - Recommend which ONE to focus on first
>
> 5. **The Reality Check Questions:**
>    - What has to be true for this forecast to hit?
>    - What's the single assumption most likely to be wrong?
>    - At what point should I panic if we're behind this plan?
>    - What leading indicators should I track weekly to know if we're on track?
>
> 6. **The Funding Requirement** — Based on this forecast and current burn rate, when do I run out of money in each scenario? Do I need to raise capital, and if so, when should I start that process?
>
> **Format:** Give me a narrative explanation plus a simple month-by-month table I can recreate in a spreadsheet.

**When to use it:** Before setting annual goals, when planning hiring, or when deciding whether to raise funding.

**Pro tip:** If you have historical data (even just 3-6 months), include actual numbers — the AI will use real trend lines instead of generic growth curves.

## 7. Partnership Evaluation Matrix

**The Prompt:**

> You are a business development strategist who has evaluated 500+ partnership opportunities and knows that most partnerships waste time without generating results.
>
> Evaluate this potential partnership:
>
> **Partnership Opportunity:**
> - Partner: [company name and what they do]
> - Type: [co-marketing / integration / reseller / referral / affiliate / joint product]
> - Their pitch: [what they're proposing]
> - What they want from me: [effort, resources, revenue share, etc.]
>
> **My Business:**
> - What I do: [your product/service]
> - Target customer: [who I sell to]
> - Current stage: [size, revenue, team size]
>
> **Evaluate across these dimensions:**
>
> 1. **Strategic Fit Score (1-10)**
>    - Do we share the same target customer?
>    - Is there actual overlap or is this a stretch?
>    - Are we complementary or competing?
>    - Scoring rationale:
>
> 2. **Value Asymmetry Analysis**
>    - What I get: [specific value — leads, distribution, credibility, etc.]
>    - What they get: [specific value]
>    - Who benefits more? By how much?
>    - Is this equitable or exploitative?
>
> 3. **The Opportunity Cost Calculation**
>    - Time required from me: [hours/week]
>    - What else could I do with that time?
>    - What's the expected ROI? (Revenue / Time Invested)
>    - Is this the best use of my next 100 hours?
>
> 4. **The Downside Risk Assessment**
>    - What's the worst case scenario?
>    - Could this damage my brand or customer relationships?
>    - What if they pivot, get acquired, or shut down?
>    - Exit strategy: How hard is it to unwind this?
>
> 5. **The Proof-of-Value Test**
>    - Before committing, what's a micro-test we could run?
>    - What success metrics would validate this partnership?
>    - How long until we know if this is working?
>
> 6. **The Negotiation Leverage Map**
>    - What do I have that they need?
>    - What's my BATNA (best alternative to this partnership)?
>    - Where can I push for better terms?
>    - Red flags to watch for in the agreement
>
> **Final Recommendation:**
> - Decision: Pursue / Pass / Renegotiate terms
> - If pursue: What specific terms should I negotiate?
> - If pass: What would need to change for this to be worth it?
> - What's the smallest possible commitment I can make to test this?

**When to use it:** Before signing any partnership agreement, affiliate deal, or co-marketing arrangement.

**Pro tip:** Include the partner's approximate size/stage (scrappy startup vs established company) — the leverage dynamics completely change the analysis.

## 8. MVP Scope Definer

**The Prompt:**

> You are a product strategist who has shipped 50+ MVPs and knows the difference between "minimum viable" and "minimum lovable." Your job is to help me cut scope ruthlessly while keeping the product viable.
>
> **Product Vision:**
> - What I want to build: [product description]
> - Problem it solves: [core pain point]
> - Target user: [who this is for]
> - Success metric: [how I'll measure if it works]
>
> **Current Feature List:**
> [List all features you're considering — be exhaustive]
>
> **Define my MVP using this framework:**
>
> 1. **The Core Value Hypothesis** — In one sentence, complete this: "Users will pay for this product if and only if it helps them [specific outcome]." Everything else is noise.
>
> 2. **The Feature Triage:**
>    Sort every feature into one of these categories:
>    - **CORE** — Without this, the product doesn't solve the problem at all
>    - **ENHANCER** — Makes the core better but isn't required for validation
>    - **DELIGHT** — Nice-to-have that improves experience
>    - **DISTRACTION** — Sounds cool but doesn't serve the core hypothesis
>
> 3. **The MVP Feature Set** — List only CORE features. For each one, explain:
>    - Why this is absolutely required for V1
>    - What's the simplest possible implementation?
>    - What manual workaround can replace automation in V1?
>
> 4. **The Fake Door Test List** — For each ENHANCER feature, design a fake door test:
>    - Add a button/link that goes to a "Coming soon, want early access?" page
>    - Measure click-through rate to validate demand before building
>
> 5. **The Technical Debt Budget** — What shortcuts can I take in V1 that I'll regret later?
>    - Rank these by: Easy to fix later (take the shortcut) vs. Architectural nightmare (do it right)
>
> 6. **The Launch Checklist** — What's the absolute minimum required to launch?
>    - Must have: [core features]
>    - Can fake manually: [what founder can do behind the scenes]
>    - Can ship later: [everything else]
>
> 7. **The Time-to-Feedback Estimate** — With this scope:
>    - How long to build? (weeks)
>    - How many users do I need to validate the hypothesis?
>    - What specific feedback would prove this works or doesn't work?
>
> **The Brutal Cut Question:** If I had to ship in 2 weeks, what would I cut? That's probably what I should cut anyway.

**When to use it:** Before writing a single line of code, or when your MVP is bloated and you need to refocus.

**Pro tip:** Include your timeline constraint ("want to launch in 6 weeks") — the AI will force cuts based on time, not just theoretical importance.

## 9. Product Positioning Statement Builder

**The Prompt:**

> You are a positioning expert who has defined category-creating positions for companies like Stripe ("payments for developers"), Figma ("design tool for teams"), and Notion ("all-in-one workspace").
>
> **My Product:**
> - What it does: [product description]
> - Who it's for: [target customer]
> - Main benefit: [what value it delivers]
> - Key differentiator: [what makes it different]
> - Current positioning (if any): [how you describe it today]
>
> **Build a positioning strategy using this framework:**
>
> 1. **The Category Decision:**
>    - Option A: Compete in an existing category (easier to explain, harder to win)
>    - Option B: Create a new category (harder to explain, easier to own)
>    - Which strategy fits my product, market maturity, and resources?
>    - Recommendation with rationale:
>
> 2. **The Positioning Statement Formula:**
>    Complete this template with 3 different variations:
>
>    "For [target customer] who [customer need/pain], [product name] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternatives], we [unique differentiator]."
>
>    - Version 1: Broad positioning (bigger market, more competition)
>    - Version 2: Niche positioning (smaller market, less competition)
>    - Version 3: Category creation (new frame entirely)
>
> 3. **The Comparison Test:**
>    For your recommended positioning, complete these customer statements:
>    - "Oh, so it's like [familiar product] but for [different use case]"
>    - "I'd use [competitor] when I need [X], but I'd use [your product] when I need [Y]"
>    - If you can't complete these clearly, the positioning is too vague
>
> 4. **The One-Word Ownership Goal:**
>    - Zoom = Video meetings
>    - Calendly = Scheduling
>    - Notion = Workspace
>    - What one word should people associate with my product in 2 years?
>
> 5. **The Messaging Hierarchy:**
>    Based on the positioning, create:
>    - **Headline** (5-10 words): The hook that stops scrolling
>    - **Subheadline** (10-20 words): Clarifies what it does and who it's for
>    - **Three Proof Points** (one sentence each): Why should they believe the claim?
>
> 6. **The Wrong Customer Filter:**
>    - Who should NOT buy this product?
>    - What use cases should we explicitly say NO to?
>    - Clear positioning requires clear exclusions
>
> 7. **The Positioning Validation Tests:**
>    - Can a 10-year-old explain what this product does after reading the positioning?
>    - Would my target customer self-identify when they hear the positioning?
>    - Does the positioning highlight a differentiator that competitors can't easily copy?
>
> **Final Output:** Give me the ONE positioning statement I should use everywhere (website, pitch deck, ads) with tactical messaging for each channel.

**When to use it:** Before building your website, raising funding, or launching paid advertising.

**Pro tip:** Include 2-3 competitor taglines so the AI can explicitly differentiate you from how they position themselves.

## 10. Market Entry Risk Assessment

**The Prompt:**

> You are a market entry strategist who has evaluated 300+ go-to-market plans and can predict which markets are attractive vs. dangerous for new entrants.
>
> **Market Entry Scenario:**
> - Market/niche: [what market you want to enter]
> - Product: [what you're selling]
> - Target customer segment: [specific buyer]
> - Your advantage: [why you think you can win]
> - Available resources: [team size, runway, budget]
>
> **Assess the risks across these dimensions:**
>
> 1. **Incumbency Risk (1-10 severity)**
>    - Who owns this market today?
>    - How entrenched are they? (Network effects, switching costs, brand moat)
>    - What would it cost them to kill my product? (acqui-hire me, copy features, price me out)
>    - Can I survive if they decide I'm a threat?
>
> 2. **The Customer Acquisition Gauntlet**
>    - What channels work in this market?
>    - What's the typical CAC for this customer segment?
>    - Are those channels already saturated?
>    - Do I have a CAC advantage or disadvantage vs. incumbents?
>    - Reality check: Can I afford to acquire customers at market rates?
>
> 3. **The Timing Test**
>    - Is this market growing, stable, or declining?
>    - Am I early (have to educate), on-time (land grab mode), or late (defensive market)?
>    - What macro trends support or threaten this market?
>    - What's the window of opportunity? (How long until it closes?)
>
> 4. **The Regulation & Compliance Minefield**
>    - Are there regulatory barriers to entry? (licensing, certifications, compliance)
>    - How long and how much does compliance cost?
>    - Are regulations getting stricter or more relaxed?
>    - Could regulation kill my business model?
>
> 5. **The Margin Reality Check**
>    - What are typical gross margins in this market?
>    - What are typical CAC and LTV multiples?
>    - Can I build a profitable business at market prices?
>    - Or am I forced into a VC-funded blitzscale or die scenario?
>
> 6. **The Competitive Response Scenario**
>    - If I gain traction, how will incumbents respond?
>    - Can they copy my features in 6 months?
>    - Can they use their advantages (brand, distribution, capital) to crush me?
>    - What's my defensibility after year 1?
>
> 7. **The Founder-Market Fit Test**
>    - Do I have unique insight, distribution, or credibility in this market?
>    - Or am I an outsider trying to break in?
>    - What unfair advantage do I have that compensates for late entry?
>
> **Risk Matrix:**
> Plot risks on two axes:
> - Likelihood (low/medium/high)
> - Impact if it happens (1-10)
> - Mitigation: For high-likelihood + high-impact risks, give me one action to reduce exposure
>
> **Final Verdict:**
> - Overall risk level: Low / Medium / High / Extremely High
> - Recommendation: Green light / Proceed with caution / Reconsider / Hard pass
> - If proceeding: What's the ONE risk I absolutely must mitigate before launch?
> - If passing: What alternative market would be safer with similar upside?

**When to use it:** Before committing significant resources to a new market, product line, or geographic expansion.

**Pro tip:** Add "Recent market events" (funding rounds, M&A, new regulations) — the AI will factor in real-time market dynamics, not just theoretical risks.

## 11. Customer Retention Strategy

**The Prompt:**

> You are a retention optimization expert who has reduced churn for 100+ SaaS and subscription businesses. You know that acquiring customers is expensive and keeping them is where real profit lives.
>
> **Current Situation:**
> - Product: [what you sell]
> - Customer type: [who buys]
> - Pricing model: [how you charge]
> - Current churn rate: [monthly % or "unknown"]
> - Average customer lifetime: [months or "unknown"]
> - Top cancellation reasons (if known): [list or "unknown"]
>
> **Build a retention strategy with this framework:**
>
> 1. **The Churn Diagnosis:**
>    Churn happens at predictable moments. Map the customer journey and identify:
>    - **Early churn** (Days 1-30): What causes buyers' remorse or failed onboarding?
>    - **Mid-life churn** (Months 2-6): When do customers stop seeing value?
>    - **Late-stage churn** (6+ months): What makes long-term customers leave?
>    - For each phase, hypothesize the top 2 churn drivers
>
> 2. **The Value Realization Roadmap:**
>    Define the "aha moment" — the exact point where a customer gets enough value that they'll stay:
>    - What action or outcome signals they "got it"?
>    - How long does it take the average customer to reach this milestone?
>    - What % of customers never reach it? (These will churn)
>    - Design 3 tactics to get more customers to the "aha moment" faster
>
> 3. **The Engagement Scoring System:**
>    Build a simple health score (0-100) based on:
>    - Product usage signals (logins, feature usage, etc.)
>    - Relationship signals (support tickets, NPS, expansion conversations)
>    - Give me the formula and define thresholds:
>      - 80-100 = Thriving (expansion opportunity)
>      - 50-79 = Healthy (maintain)
>      - 20-49 = At risk (intervention needed)
>      - 0-19 = Churning (save or let go)
>
> 4. **The Intervention Playbook:**
>    For each health score segment, design a specific intervention:
>    - **At Risk** (50-20 score): What outreach, offer, or support can rescue them?
>    - **Churning** (20-0 score): What's the Hail Mary save offer? (pause subscription, discount, white-glove support)
>    - **Thriving** (80-100 score): How do we expand revenue? (upsell, cross-sell, referral ask)
>
> 5. **The Win-Back Campaign:**
>    For customers who already churned:
>    - Segment 1: Churned due to price → Offer?
>    - Segment 2: Churned due to missing features → What's changed?
>    - Segment 3: Churned due to lack of usage → What educational content or onboarding would help?
>    - Design 3 win-back email sequences (one per segment)
>
> 6. **The Retention Metrics Dashboard:**
>    What should I track weekly to improve retention?
>    - Leading indicators (predict churn before it happens)
>    - Lagging indicators (measure churn after the fact)
>    - For each metric, give me the target benchmark for a healthy business
>
> 7. **The Expansion Revenue Engine:**
>    Retained customers should grow in value over time:
>    - What triggers an upsell conversation? (usage thresholds, team growth, etc.)
>    - What's the logical next product or tier?
>    - Design a net revenue retention (NRR) target: What % of customers should expand vs. churn?
>
> **30-Day Action Plan:** Give me the top 3 retention initiatives I should implement this month, ranked by impact and effort.

**When to use it:** When churn is above 5% monthly, when CAC payback takes too long, or when you need to improve unit economics fast.

**Pro tip:** If you have access to churn data, include the top 3 cancellation reasons customers give — the AI will design retention tactics that directly address those pain points.

## 12. Investor Pitch Deck Outliner

**The Prompt:**

> You are a pitch deck consultant who has helped 200+ founders raise $500M+ in funding. You know what investors actually care about vs. what founders think they care about.
>
> **Fundraising Context:**
> - Company: [name and one-sentence description]

> - Raising: [$ amount]
> - Traction: [revenue, users, growth rate, or "pre-launch"]
> - Market: [industry and TAM size if known]
>
> **Build a pitch deck outline with these slides:**
>
> 1. **SLIDE 1 — Company Intro**
>    - One-sentence hook that makes investors lean in
>    - Format: "[Company] is [what you do] for [who], solving [expensive problem]."
>    - Example: "Stripe is payments infrastructure for developers, eliminating weeks of integration work."
>
> 2. **SLIDE 2 — The Problem**
>    - What's the painful, expensive, urgent problem?
>    - Who has this problem and how much does it cost them today?
>    - Why hasn't this been solved yet?
>    - Make the investor feel the pain personally
>
> 3. **SLIDE 3 — The Solution**
>    - How does your product solve the problem?
>    - Show don't tell: Include a visual (screenshot, diagram, or flow)
>    - Why is this solution 10x better than alternatives, not 10% better?
>
> 4. **SLIDE 4 — Market Size (TAM/SAM/SOM)**
>    - TAM (Total Addressable Market): How big is the entire market?
>    - SAM (Serviceable Available Market): How much could you realistically capture?
>    - SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): What's your 3-year revenue target?
>    - Show bottoms-up math, not top-down "1% of a giant market" BS
>
> 5. **SLIDE 5 — Product Demo / How It Works**
>    - Walk through the core user flow in 3-5 steps
>    - Use visuals, not bullet points
>    - Highlight the "magic moment" where value is delivered
>
> 6. **SLIDE 6 — Traction**
>    - What proof do you have that this works?
>    - Revenue, users, growth rate, retention, marquee customers
>    - Show a "hockey stick" growth chart if possible
>    - If pre-revenue: LOIs, waitlist size, pilot customers, or founder credibility
>
> 7. **SLIDE 7 — Business Model**
>    - How do you make money?
>    - What do you charge and why?
>    - Unit economics: CAC, LTV, gross margin, payback period
>    - Path to profitability (even if it's 5 years out)
>
> 8. **SLIDE 8 — Go-to-Market Strategy**
>    - How will you acquire customers?
>    - What channels work today and which will you add at scale?
>    - What's your unfair distribution advantage?
>    - Why won't customer acquisition costs skyrocket as you scale?
>
> 9. **SLIDE 9 — Competition & Differentiation**
>    - Who are the alternatives? (Direct competitors, indirect solutions, status quo)
>    - Why will you win? (Not "better product" — what structural advantage do you have?)
>    - Show a 2x2 matrix positioning you uniquely
>
> 10. **SLIDE 10 — Team**
>     - Why is this team uniquely qualified to win this market?
>     - Highlight relevant expertise, domain credibility, or previous exits
>     - Show you can build, sell, and scale
>     - If missing key roles, acknowledge what you'll hire for
>
> 11. **SLIDE 11 — Financial Projections**
>     - 3-year revenue projection (conservative, base, aggressive)
>     - Key assumptions (CAC, LTV, conversion rates)
>     - What milestones will this raise unlock?
>
> 12. **SLIDE 12 — The Ask**
>     - How much are you raising?
>     - What will you use it for? (Breakdown: 60% engineering, 30% GTM, 10% ops)
>     - What milestones will you hit with this capital?
>     - What's the next round look like? (Give them the vision for 18-24 months out)
>
> **For each slide, provide:**
> - Slide title
> - 2-3 bullet points of what to include
> - One "investor objection" this slide must preemptively answer
> - One "red flag to avoid" (common mistakes founders make on this slide)
>
> **Bonus: The Narrative Arc**
> Write a 2-minute verbal pitch script that connects these slides into a compelling story. Investors decide emotionally, then justify rationally. Give them a narrative, not just data.

**When to use it:** Before creating your first pitch deck or when refining an existing deck that isn't landing with investors.

**Pro tip:** Include your "unfair advantage" (unique insight, proprietary tech, exclusive partnership, etc.) — the AI will weave this throughout the deck as your key differentiator.
