# Content Creation Prompts

## 1. Blog Post Architecture

**The Prompt:**

> I need to write a blog post about [topic] for [target audience]. Before I write anything, build me an architecture:
>
> 1. **3 possible angles** — give me three different approaches to this topic, each with a working title. One should be contrarian, one educational, one story-driven.
> 2. **For my chosen angle** (or recommend one): create a detailed outline with:
>    - A hook opening (first 2 sentences that stop the scroll)
>    - 5-7 subheadings that create a logical flow
>    - For each section: the key point, an example or data point to include, and the transition to the next section
>    - A conclusion that drives action, not just summarizes
> 3. **SEO guidance:** Primary keyword, 5 secondary keywords, suggested meta description (under 155 characters)
> 4. **Internal linking opportunities:** What related topics should I link to?
>
> Target word count: [number]. Reading level: [casual/professional/technical].

**When to use it:** At the start of any blog post, especially when you're staring at a blank page and don't know where to begin.

**Pro tip:** After getting the outline, ask "Rewrite the hook as if you only had 280 characters to make someone click" — that usually produces a better opening.

## 2. Newsletter That People Actually Read

**The Prompt:**

> Write a newsletter edition for my [industry/niche] audience. Here's what I want to cover:
>
> - Main topic: [topic or recent event]
> - My take/opinion: [your angle, if you have one]
> - Tone: [casual/professional/witty/authoritative]
>
> Structure it as:
> 1. **Subject line:** Give me 5 options. One curiosity-driven, one benefit-driven, one pattern-interrupt, one question, one number-driven.
> 2. **Opening hook:** First 2 lines that make someone glad they opened this email (no "Happy Tuesday!" generic intros)
> 3. **Main content:** The insight, opinion, or lesson — keep it to 300 words max. Make every sentence earn its place.
> 4. **The "so what":** Why this matters to the reader specifically, not just in general
> 5. **One actionable takeaway:** Something they can do today, not someday
> 6. **Sign-off:** Something that feels human, not corporate
>
> Write it in first person. Make it sound like a smart friend sharing something over coffee, not a brand broadcasting a message.

**When to use it:** When writing your weekly or monthly newsletter and you want it to feel personal, not templated.

**Pro tip:** Paste in your last 2-3 newsletters and ask the AI to "match my voice" before generating — the output will sound much more like you.

## 3. Social Caption Batch Creator

**The Prompt:**

> Generate a week's worth (7 posts) of social media captions for my [business type]. My brand voice is [describe: casual, professional, witty, bold, etc.].
>
> For each post, provide:
> - **Platform:** [Instagram/LinkedIn/Twitter — specify or do all three]
> - **Caption:** Written with platform-specific best practices (length, tone, format)
> - **Hook:** The first line that shows up before "...see more"
> - **CTA:** A natural call-to-action that doesn't feel salesy
> - **Hashtags:** 5-10 relevant hashtags (Instagram only)
> - **Best posting time:** Suggested day/time based on platform norms
>
> Mix of content types across the week:
> - 2 value/educational posts
> - 2 engagement/question posts
> - 1 behind-the-scenes/personal post
> - 1 social proof/testimonial-style post
> - 1 promotional post (soft sell)
>
> Topic areas to pull from: [list 3-5 topics relevant to your business]

**When to use it:** During your weekly content planning session when you need to batch-create social content efficiently.

**Pro tip:** Add "Each caption should work as a standalone piece — no post should reference or depend on another" so you can rearrange or skip posts freely.

## 4. Video Script Framework

**The Prompt:**

> Write a script for a [length]-minute video about [topic]. Platform: [YouTube/TikTok/Instagram Reels/course content].
>
> **Structure:**
> 1. **Hook (first 3 seconds):** The line that stops them from scrolling. Give me 3 options.
> 2. **Context (10 seconds):** Why they should keep watching. Establish credibility without bragging.
> 3. **Main content:** Break into [3-5] clear sections, each with:
>    - The point (one sentence)
>    - The explanation (conversational, not lecture-style)
>    - An example or analogy that makes it stick
>    - A visual/B-roll suggestion (what should be on screen while saying this)
> 4. **Callback:** Reference the hook to create a satisfying arc
> 5. **CTA:** What I want them to do next (subscribe, comment, visit link)
>
> Write it conversationally — the way someone actually talks, not the way someone writes. Include [PAUSE], [EMPHASIS], and [CUT TO] markers for editing.
>
> My style: [describe — fast-paced, calm, humorous, educational, etc.]

**When to use it:** Before filming any video content, whether it's a 30-second reel or a 15-minute YouTube video.

**Pro tip:** Read the script out loud before filming. If you stumble on a phrase, ask the AI to "rewrite this section for spoken delivery, not written delivery."

## 5. Headline Swipe File Generator

**The Prompt:**

> I need headlines for [content piece: blog post/email/ad/landing page] about [topic]. My target audience is [description].
>
> Generate 25 headlines using these proven formulas (label which formula each one uses):
>
> 1. **How-to:** "How to [achieve result] without [common pain]"
> 2. **Number list:** "[Number] ways to [achieve outcome]"
> 3. **Question:** A question they can't help but want answered
> 4. **Contrarian:** Challenge a common belief in the industry
> 5. **Urgency:** Time-sensitive or scarcity-driven
> 6. **Social proof:** Leveraging results or authority
> 7. **Curiosity gap:** Opens a loop they need to close
> 8. **Direct benefit:** Plain statement of what they'll get
> 9. **Negative angle:** "Stop doing [common mistake]" or "Why [thing] is failing you"
> 10. **Story hook:** Teases a narrative
>
> For each headline, rate it 1-5 on: clarity, curiosity, and click-worthiness. Star your top 5 recommendations.

**When to use it:** Whenever you need a title, subject line, or heading and don't want to use the first mediocre idea that pops into your head.

**Pro tip:** Take the top 3 headlines and ask "Combine the best elements of these three into one ultimate headline" for a version that's stronger than any individual option.

## 6. Content Repurposing Machine

**The Prompt:**

> Here's a piece of content I created: [paste your blog post, video transcript, podcast summary, or newsletter].
>
> Repurpose this into all of the following formats:
>
> 1. **Twitter/X thread:** 7-10 tweets that tell a cohesive story. First tweet is the hook. Last tweet is a CTA.
> 2. **LinkedIn post:** Professional but not boring. Include a personal reflection or hot take.
> 3. **Instagram carousel:** 8-10 slides. Give me the text for each slide (headline + 1-2 sentences). First slide is a scroll-stopper.
> 4. **Email newsletter snippet:** 150-word version I can include in my weekly email.
> 5. **Pull quotes:** 5 standalone sentences from the original that work as shareable graphics.
> 6. **Short-form video script:** 60-second script hitting the key insight from the content.
>
> Maintain my original voice and key message. Each format should feel native to its platform, not like a lazy copy-paste.

**When to use it:** After publishing any piece of long-form content, to extract maximum value from the work you already did.

**Pro tip:** Don't repurpose everything. Ask "Which of these formats has the highest potential for this specific piece of content?" and focus your energy there.

## 7. SEO Content Brief Builder

**The Prompt:**

> Create a comprehensive SEO content brief for the keyword: "[your target keyword]"
>
> Include:
> 1. **Search intent analysis:** Is this informational, transactional, navigational, or commercial? What does someone searching this actually want?
> 2. **Content format recommendation:** Should this be a listicle, how-to guide, comparison, review, or something else? Why?
> 3. **Suggested word count:** Based on what typically ranks for this type of query
> 4. **Outline with H2s and H3s:** A full content structure optimized for featured snippets and readability
> 5. **Semantic keywords:** 15-20 related terms and phrases I should naturally include
> 6. **Questions to answer:** 5-7 "People Also Ask" style questions this content should address
> 7. **Internal/external linking suggestions:** What should I link to and from?
> 8. **Content differentiation:** What angle can I take that the current top 5 results aren't covering?
>
> Make this brief detailed enough that any competent writer could produce a top-ranking article from it.

**When to use it:** Before writing any content that you want to rank in search engines.

**Pro tip:** Combine this with the Blog Post Architecture prompt — use this one for the SEO strategy, then that one for the creative structure.

## 8. Email Copy That Converts

**The Prompt:**

> Write a [type: promotional/welcome sequence/abandoned cart/re-engagement] email for [product/service].
>
> **Context:**
> - Audience: [who they are]
> - Goal: [what you want them to do]
> - Offer: [what you're promoting, if applicable]
> - Brand voice: [describe your tone]
>
> **Deliver:**
> 1. **Subject line:** 5 options with predicted open rate ranking (best to worst). Keep under 50 characters.
> 2. **Preview text:** The snippet that shows next to the subject line. 3 options.
> 3. **Email body:**
>    - Opening line that creates immediate relevance (no "Hi [Name], I hope you're well")
>    - Body copy that builds desire using PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution) or AIDA framework. Label which one you're using.
>    - Social proof element (testimonial, stat, or result)
>    - Clear, single CTA button text (3-5 words)
>    - P.S. line (these get read more than body copy)
> 4. **Plain text version:** A simplified version for accessibility and deliverability
>
> Total email length: under 200 words. Every sentence must earn its place.

**When to use it:** When writing any marketing email, especially if your open rates or click rates are below industry averages.

**Pro tip:** Write the P.S. line first, then work backward — the P.S. often captures the core message better than the body.

## 9. Landing Page Copy Blueprint

**The Prompt:**

> Write landing page copy for [product/service]. Price: [amount]. Target: [audience].
>
> Provide copy for each section of the page, in order:
>
> 1. **Hero section:**
>    - Headline (10 words max — clear beats clever)
>    - Subheadline (explains the how or the proof)
>    - CTA button text
>    - Social proof line ("Join 5,000+ marketers" style)
> 2. **Problem section:** 3 pain points described in the customer's own language, not marketing speak
> 3. **Solution section:** How the product solves each pain point — features translated into benefits
> 4. **Social proof section:** Suggest 3 types of proof to include (testimonials, logos, stats, case studies) with example text
> 5. **Pricing/offer section:** How to present the price to make it feel like a no-brainer. Include an anchor price or value comparison.
> 6. **FAQ section:** 5 objection-handling FAQs with answers
> 7. **Final CTA section:** A different headline and CTA from the hero — this is for people who read the whole page
>
> Write two versions: one for a cold audience (never heard of me) and one for a warm audience (already on my email list).

**When to use it:** When building or redesigning any sales or product page.

**Pro tip:** After getting the copy, ask "Read this landing page as a skeptical customer. What makes you hesitate? Now fix those hesitations."

## 10. Storytelling Framework Activator

**The Prompt:**

> I need to tell a story to [sell/teach/inspire] my audience about [topic/product/lesson]. Help me build a compelling narrative:
>
> **The raw material:** [describe the situation, experience, or case study you want to turn into a story]
>
> **Build the story using this framework:**
> 1. **The hook:** Start in the middle of the action or with a surprising statement. No "Once upon a time" or "Let me tell you about..."
> 2. **The setup:** Who is the character? What did they want? Why should the audience care?
> 3. **The conflict:** What stood in the way? Make the obstacle feel real and relatable.
> 4. **The turning point:** What changed? What insight, decision, or event shifted everything?
> 5. **The resolution:** What happened as a result? Be specific — use numbers, timelines, or tangible outcomes.
> 6. **The lesson:** What should the audience take away? Connect it back to them, not just the character.
> 7. **The bridge:** How does this story connect to what you're selling, teaching, or asking them to do next?
>
> Write it in [first person/third person]. Keep it under [word count]. Make me feel something.

**When to use it:** When you have a great case study, personal experience, or customer story but can't figure out how to tell it compellingly.

**Pro tip:** The best business stories follow the pattern: "I used to believe X. Then Y happened. Now I believe Z." Start there.

## 11. Content Calendar Architect

**The Prompt:**

> Build me a 30-day content calendar for [business/brand].
>
> **Context:**
> - Platforms: [list platforms]
> - Posting frequency: [how often per platform]
> - Content pillars: [3-5 topics you consistently cover]
> - Upcoming events/launches: [anything time-sensitive]
> - Audience: [who follows you]
>
> **For each day, provide:**
> - Platform
> - Content type (educational, entertaining, promotional, engagement, behind-the-scenes)
> - Topic/angle
> - Working headline or hook
> - Format (text, image, video, carousel, story, reel)
> - Notes on timing or context
>
> **Rules:**
> - No more than 20% promotional content
> - Each content pillar gets roughly equal coverage
> - Include engagement posts (questions, polls, debates) at least twice per week
> - Build toward the launch/event if one exists
> - Vary formats — don't do 5 carousels in a row
>
> Present as a table organized by week.

**When to use it:** At the start of each month, or when you're tired of improvising content daily.

**Pro tip:** Add "Include one 'experimental' post per week that's outside my normal content style" to prevent your feed from getting predictable.

## 12. Authority Article Outliner

**The Prompt:**

> I want to write a definitive, authoritative article about [topic] — the kind of piece people bookmark and reference. My expertise is in [your background].
>
> Help me plan this:
>
> 1. **Unique angle:** What perspective can I bring that generic articles on this topic don't have? Consider my experience in [background].
> 2. **Research plan:** What data, studies, or examples should I reference to build credibility? List 5-7 specific things to look up.
> 3. **Detailed outline:**
>    - Introduction that establishes why existing advice on this topic is wrong or incomplete
>    - 5-7 main sections, each with a clear thesis, supporting evidence, and practical takeaway
>    - Each section should build on the previous one (not just a random list)
>    - Conclusion that gives a clear "now what" for the reader
> 4. **Original frameworks:** Create 1-2 original frameworks, models, or matrices I can introduce in this article (things people will screenshot and share)
> 5. **Quotable lines:** Write 5 sentences that are designed to be highlighted, shared, or pulled as quotes
>
> This should be 2,000-3,000 words when finished. It's not a listicle — it's a thought leadership piece.

**When to use it:** When you want to create a flagship content piece that establishes your expertise and drives long-term traffic.

**Pro tip:** Ask "What would make an expert in [field] share this article?" to pressure-test whether it's genuinely adding to the conversation or just repackaging existing ideas.
