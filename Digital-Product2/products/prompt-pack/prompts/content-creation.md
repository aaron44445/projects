# Content Creation Prompts

## 1. Blog Post Architecture

**The Prompt:**

> You are an expert content strategist who builds blog posts that rank and convert.
>
> I need a complete blog post structure for: [TOPIC]
>
> My target reader is: [SPECIFIC AUDIENCE — be detailed about their pain points, experience level, and what they're trying to achieve]
>
> Primary goal of this post: [Rank for [KEYWORD] / Build email list / Sell [PRODUCT] / Establish authority]
>
> Create a detailed outline including:
>
> 1. **Title Options** — Give me 5 headline variations optimized for [SEO / Curiosity / Social shares]. Include the emotional trigger each one uses.
>
> 2. **Opening Hook** (2-3 sentences): Start with a surprising fact, provocative question, or relatable scenario — never start with "In today's world" or similar clichés
>
> 3. **Content Architecture** — Break down 5-7 H2 sections with:
>    - The exact H2 text (keyword-aware but human-written)
>    - 2-3 bullet points of what each section must cover
>    - Specific examples, data points, or stories to include
>    - Transition strategy to the next section
>
> 4. **Engagement Mechanisms** — Identify 3 places in the post to add:
>    - A scroll-stopping visual or quote card
>    - An interactive element (self-assessment, calculator, checklist)
>    - A pattern interrupt (controversial take, myth-busting, case study twist)
>
> 5. **Conversion Architecture** — Design the CTA strategy:
>    - Soft CTA placement (where and what type)
>    - Primary CTA (exact copy + placement)
>    - Exit-intent offer (if applicable)
>
> 6. **SEO Foundation** — List:
>    - Primary keyword + 4-5 semantic variants to weave in naturally
>    - 3 internal link opportunities (describe the anchor context)
>    - Meta description (155 characters, includes keyword + curiosity gap)
>
> Make this outline so detailed I could hand it to any writer and they'd produce a 10/10 post.

**When to use it:** Use this when you need a comprehensive blog post plan that handles strategy, structure, SEO, and conversion in one shot instead of writing aimlessly.

**Pro tip:** Fill in the "target reader" section with extreme specificity (job title, exact frustration, what they Googled) — the more detailed your reader description, the better the outline matches real search intent.

## 2. Newsletter That People Actually Read

**The Prompt:**

> You are a newsletter strategist who has studied the top 1% of newsletters that maintain 50%+ open rates and single-digit unsubscribe rates.
>
> Help me write my next newsletter issue.
>
> **Newsletter Context:**
> - My audience: [WHO THEY ARE + WHAT THEY CARE ABOUT]
> - Newsletter tone: [Professional and data-driven / Casual and story-driven / Contrarian and spicy / Educational and actionable]
> - Typical length: [500 words / 1000 words / 1500+ words]
> - Send frequency: [Daily / Weekly / Biweekly]
>
> **This Issue's Content:**
> Main topic/story I want to cover: [TOPIC]
>
> Key insight or takeaway: [THE ONE THING readers should remember]
>
> **Create a complete newsletter structure:**
>
> 1. **Subject Line Formula** — Give me 5 subject line options using these high-performing patterns:
>    - Curiosity gap (promise without revealing)
>    - Specificity (numbers, data, named examples)
>    - Contrarian (challenge conventional wisdom)
>    - Urgency (timely, trending, "this just happened")
>    - Personal (vulnerable, behind-the-scenes, lesson learned)
>
>    For each, explain WHY it works and predict open rate potential.
>
> 2. **Opening Line** — Write 3 different opening sentences. Each should accomplish one of these:
>    - Make a bold claim that demands a response
>    - Tell a micro-story (2-3 sentences max) that illustrates the main point
>    - Ask a question that 80% of readers will answer "yes" to
>
> 3. **Core Content Structure** — Organize the main content using ONE of these proven frameworks (choose the best fit):
>    - **Problem → Agitation → Solution** (for educational content)
>    - **Story → Lesson → Application** (for experience-driven content)
>    - **Observation → Analysis → Prediction** (for trend/news content)
>    - **Mistake → Consequence → Correction** (for lessons learned)
>
>    Write the full structure with subheadings and key points for each section.
>
> 4. **Retention Devices** — Add 2-3 of these to keep readers engaged:
>    - A surprising data point or counterintuitive fact
>    - A "this reminds me of" connection to something unexpected
>    - A one-sentence case study or named example
>    - A visual description (describe what the reader should imagine)
>
> 5. **The Close** — Write two closing options:
>    - **Soft close**: Thought-provoking question or reflection (no hard ask)
>    - **Action close**: One clear, specific thing the reader should do next
>
> 6. **P.S. Section** — Write a P.S. that does ONE of these:
>    - Teases next week's topic
>    - Shares a quick personal update or behind-the-scenes
>    - Includes a soft pitch or relevant resource
>    - Poses a question that encourages reply
>
> Make this newsletter feel like it came from a human who respects my time, not a content robot.

**When to use it:** Use this before writing any newsletter issue when you want a strategic structure that keeps readers engaged from subject line to P.S. instead of winging it and losing people halfway through.

**Pro tip:** Always choose ONE framework for the core content structure and stick to it — mixing frameworks creates confusion and tanks readability.

## 3. Social Caption Batch Creator

**The Prompt:**

> You are a social media strategist who specializes in creating scroll-stopping captions that drive engagement and build audience.
>
> I need to batch-create social media captions for the next [TIMEFRAME: week / 2 weeks / month].
>
> **Account Details:**
> - Platform: [Instagram / LinkedIn / Twitter/X / TikTok / Facebook]
> - My niche/industry: [SPECIFIC NICHE]
> - Target audience: [WHO THEY ARE + WHAT THEY STRUGGLE WITH]
> - Account goal: [Grow followers / Drive traffic / Build authority / Generate leads / Sell products]
> - Brand voice: [Describe in 3-5 adjectives: educational, witty, vulnerable, no-BS, inspirational, etc.]
>
> **Content Themes I Post About:**
> 1. [THEME 1 — e.g., productivity tips, industry news, personal journey, etc.]
> 2. [THEME 2]
> 3. [THEME 3]
>
> **Create [NUMBER: 7-20] captions** using this high-performing structure for EACH caption:
>
> **Hook (First Line):**
> - Must work in the feed preview (first 125 characters)
> - Use one of these proven patterns: Bold claim, Surprising stat, Relatable pain point, Contrarian take, Story opening, Direct question, "Here's what nobody tells you about X"
>
> **Body (Main Content):**
> - Break into short paragraphs (2-3 lines max per paragraph)
> - Use ONE clear structure: Listicle (3-5 points) / Story arc / Problem-solution / Before-after / Myth-busting
> - Include at least one specificity marker (number, name, time, place)
> - Add one vulnerability moment or personal insight
>
> **CTA (Call to Action):**
> - Engagement CTA: Ask a specific question readers can answer in comments
> - OR Traffic CTA: Direct to link in bio with clear benefit
> - OR Tag CTA: "Tag someone who needs to hear this"
>
> **Format Each Caption Like This:**
>
> ---
> **Caption [NUMBER] — [THEME]**
> [Platform-specific optimization note: Hashtag strategy for IG, tag suggestions for LinkedIn, thread potential for X]
>
> [FULL CAPTION TEXT]
>
> **Best time to post:** [Day/time based on content type]
> **Pair with:** [Suggest visual type — carousel, single image, video, text graphic]
>
> ---
>
> **Distribution Strategy:**
> - Organize captions by theme (so I can batch-create visuals)
> - Vary the hook patterns across all captions (no repetitive openings)
> - Include a mix of content types: 60% educational/valuable, 30% engaging/entertaining, 10% promotional
> - Mark which 2-3 captions have the highest viral potential
>
> Make these captions feel authentic to my voice while following proven engagement formulas.

**When to use it:** Use this when you need to create a week or month of social content in one session instead of staring at a blank caption box every single day.

**Pro tip:** After getting all captions, ask the AI to "rewrite caption [NUMBER] in 3 different voice variations" for any that feel off-brand — this fine-tunes the tone without starting from scratch.

## 4. Video Script Framework

**The Prompt:**

> You are a video script strategist who specializes in writing scripts that hold attention, deliver value, and drive action.
>
> I need a complete video script.
>
> **Video Details:**
> - Platform: [YouTube / TikTok / Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts / LinkedIn / VSL/Sales video]
> - Video type: [Tutorial / Explainer / Story / Review / Opinion/Commentary / Sales/Promo]
> - Target length: [Under 60 sec / 60-90 sec / 3-5 min / 5-10 min / 10-20 min / 20+ min]
> - Topic: [WHAT THE VIDEO IS ABOUT]
> - Viewer goal: [What should the viewer be able to do/know/feel after watching?]
>
> **Audience Context:**
> - Who they are: [SPECIFIC DESCRIPTION]
> - What they already know: [Their current knowledge level on this topic]
> - Why they're watching: [The problem they're trying to solve or outcome they want]
>
> **Create a complete video script with these sections:**
>
> **1. HOOK (First 3-8 seconds)**
> Write the exact opening line + visual direction.
> - Must answer: "Why should I keep watching?"
> - Use a pattern: Make a promise, Show the end result, Challenge a belief, Ask a question they can't ignore, Start mid-action/story
> - Include: [On-screen text suggestion if applicable]
> - Retention goal: Stop the scroll and commit to watching
>
> **2. PROMISE/SETUP (Next 5-15 seconds)**
> Bridge from hook to content.
> - Clearly state what this video will deliver
> - Add credibility marker (your experience, result, unique angle)
> - Set expectations (time, complexity, outcome)
> - Transition smoothly into main content
>
> **3. MAIN CONTENT (Core of the video)**
> Structure the teaching/story/explanation using ONE of these frameworks:
>
> *For Educational/Tutorial:*
> - Problem → Why it matters → Solution (step-by-step) → Common mistake to avoid → Quick recap
>
> *For Story/Experience:*
> - Setup (situation) → Conflict (what went wrong) → Turning point → Resolution → Lesson/takeaway
>
> *For Opinion/Commentary:*
> - The mainstream belief → Why that's incomplete/wrong → My contrarian take → Evidence/example → Implications
>
> Write the full script with:
> - **[VISUAL]** cues in brackets for what should be on screen
> - **[B-ROLL]** suggestions for supporting footage
> - **[ON-SCREEN TEXT]** for key points to reinforce
> - Natural speech patterns (conversational, not robotic)
> - Built-in pattern interrupts every 30-45 seconds (visual change, tonal shift, new info)
>
> **4. RETENTION MECHANISMS**
> Mark where to insert these throughout the script:
> - **Callback**: Reference the hook/promise ("Remember when I said...")
> - **Curiosity loop**: Tease something coming later ("More on that in a second...")
> - **Proof point**: Quick example, stat, or case study
> - **Relatability moment**: "You know when..." or "This happened to me..."
>
> **5. CLOSE & CTA**
> Write two closing options:
>
> *Option A — Engagement CTA:*
> - Quick recap of key point
> - Specific question for comments
> - Suggested next video (if applicable)
>
> *Option B — Action CTA:*
> - One clear next step
> - Where to go (link, profile, product)
> - Benefit of taking action NOW
>
> **6. METADATA PACKAGE**
> - Title: 3 options (optimize for platform algorithm + clickability)
> - Thumbnail text: 3-5 words max that create curiosity gap
> - Description: First 150 characters (what shows before "read more")
> - Tags/Hashtags: Platform-appropriate list
>
> **7. TIMING BREAKDOWN**
> Provide timestamp structure:
> - 0:00-0:03 Hook
> - 0:03-0:15 Promise/Setup
> - 0:15-X:XX Main Content (broken into logical segments)
> - X:XX-End Close & CTA
>
> Write this script so I can read it naturally on camera or send it to a video editor and they'll know exactly what to create.

**When to use it:** Use this before filming any video when you want a complete script with visual direction, retention hooks, and CTAs instead of rambling on camera and hoping it works.

**Pro tip:** Always specify your target length — the AI will structure retention mechanisms and pacing differently for a 60-second Reel vs. a 10-minute YouTube video.

## 5. Headline Swipe File Generator

**The Prompt:**

> You are a direct response copywriter who has studied decades of high-converting headlines across ads, landing pages, emails, and editorial content.
>
> I need a swipe file of headline formulas customized for my specific offer/topic.
>
> **What I'm Promoting:**
> - Offer/Topic: [PRODUCT, SERVICE, ARTICLE TOPIC, OR IDEA]
> - Target audience: [WHO + THEIR MAIN DESIRE OR PAIN POINT]
> - Unique mechanism or angle: [What makes this different/better/new]
> - Desired action: [What should they do after reading the headline: Click, Buy, Read, Subscribe, Share]
>
> **Create 25 headlines** organized by proven psychological frameworks:
>
> **CURIOSITY HEADLINES (5 headlines)**
> Pattern: Create information gap without revealing the answer
> - "The [Surprising Thing] That [Desirable Outcome]"
> - "What [Authority Figure] Knows About [Topic] That You Don't"
> - "The [Number] [Thing] Nobody Tells You About [Topic]"
> - "[Unexpected A] Is The New [Expected B]"
> - "Why [Counterintuitive Claim]"
>
> **BENEFIT-DRIVEN HEADLINES (5 headlines)**
> Pattern: Lead with the end result, not the process
> - "How To [Achieve Specific Outcome] In [Timeframe] Without [Common Obstacle]"
> - "Get [Specific Result] Even If [Limiting Belief]"
> - "[Do Desirable Thing] Like [Aspirational Comparison]"
> - "The [Simple Method] For [Achieving Goal] That [Impressive Qualifier]"
> - "[Number] Ways To [Accomplish Goal] Starting [Timeframe]"
>
> **FEAR/PROBLEM-FOCUSED HEADLINES (5 headlines)**
> Pattern: Agitate the pain point before hinting at solution
> - "Are You Making These [Number] [Mistake Type] Mistakes?"
> - "What Everyone Gets Wrong About [Topic]"
> - "The [Common Approach] Trap (And How To Avoid It)"
> - "[Negative Outcome] Is Killing Your [Desired Result]"
> - "Why Your [Current Solution] Isn't Working"
>
> **SPECIFICITY HEADLINES (5 headlines)**
> Pattern: Use concrete numbers, names, or data points
> - "[Specific Number] [Things] That [Generated Specific Result]"
> - "How [Named Person/Company] [Achieved Specific Outcome] In [Exact Timeframe]"
> - "From [Negative Starting Point] To [Positive End Point] In [Specific Time]"
> - "[Exact Number]% Of [Audience] Don't Know This About [Topic]"
> - "I [Did Specific Thing] For [Number] [Time Period] — Here's What Happened"
>
> **CONTRARIAN/BOLD CLAIM HEADLINES (5 headlines)**
> Pattern: Challenge conventional wisdom or make controversial statement
> - "Why [Popular Belief] Is Wrong About [Topic]"
> - "[Conventional Wisdom] Is Dead. Here's What Works Now."
> - "Forget [Common Advice] — Do This Instead"
> - "The Uncomfortable Truth About [Topic]"
> - "[Controversial Statement] And Why That's Actually Good News"
>
> **For each headline, include:**
> - ✅ **Why it works**: The psychological trigger it uses
> - 📊 **Best use case**: Where this headline performs best (ads, email subject, blog title, social post)
> - ⚡ **Power rating**: Rate conviction/clickability on 1-10 scale
>
> **Also provide:**
> 1. **Top 3 Headlines**: Which 3 have the highest combination of curiosity + clarity + credibility
> 2. **A/B Test Pairs**: Suggest 3 pairs to split test (explain why each matchup is worth testing)
> 3. **Red Flags**: Which headlines might trigger skepticism or create wrong expectations
>
> Make these headlines specific enough to use immediately, not generic templates.

**When to use it:** Use this when you need headline options for a launch, campaign, or piece of content and you want to test multiple psychological angles instead of guessing what will work.

**Pro tip:** Take the top 3 headlines and ask the AI to "create 5 more variations of headline [NUMBER] using the same psychological framework but different word choices" to get even more options in that winning style.

> - Length: [Word count / Duration]
> - Target audience: [WHO IT'S FOR]
>
> [PASTE THE FULL CONTENT OR DETAILED SUMMARY HERE]
>
> **Repurpose this into the following formats:**
>
> [Select the formats you want — delete the ones you don't need]
>
> **1. TWITTER/X THREAD (8-12 tweets)**

> - Each tweet is a standalone insight (quotable)
> - Built-in curiosity gaps to keep reading

> - Extract 5-7 questions answered by the original content
> - Rewrite as Q&A format
> - SEO-friendly structure (each question as H2)
> - Internal link opportunities
>

> - **Publishing order**: What should go out first, second, third (and why)
> - **Timing gaps**: How many days between each format to avoid audience fatigue
> - **Cross-promotion**: How each format should reference the others
> - **Tracking**: What metrics to watch for each format
>
> **For each repurposed piece, include:**
> - ✅ Estimated time to create
> - 📊 Expected engagement level (low/medium/high) for my audience
> - 🎯 Primary goal (traffic, engagement, authority, leads)
>
> Turn my one piece of content into a full content ecosystem.

**When to use it:** Use this after creating any substantial piece of content (blog post, video, podcast) when you want to squeeze 10-20 pieces of content from one core idea instead of letting it die after one post.

**Pro tip:** Don't repurpose everything at once — use the AI's "publishing order" recommendation to space out releases over 2-4 weeks so you're not competing with yourself.

## 7. SEO Content Brief Builder

**The Prompt:**

> You are an SEO content strategist who creates comprehensive content briefs that help writers create first-page ranking content on the first draft.
>
> I need a complete SEO content brief for a target keyword.
>
> **Target Keyword:** [EXACT KEYWORD PHRASE]
>
> **Additional Context:**
> - My website/brand: [YOUR SITE]
> - Domain authority (if known): [DA SCORE or "new site" or "established"]
> - Content type: [Blog post / Guide / Comparison / Review / Listicle / How-to]
> - Business goal: [Rank and capture traffic / Support product sales / Build email list / Establish authority]
>
> **Create a comprehensive SEO content brief with these sections:**
>
> **1. SEARCH INTENT ANALYSIS**
> Based on this keyword, determine:
> - Primary intent: [Informational / Navigational / Commercial / Transactional]
> - What the searcher actually wants: [Describe in plain language what problem they're trying to solve]
> - Where they are in the buyer journey: [Awareness / Consideration / Decision]
> - Implied questions they need answered (list 5-7 questions)
>
> **2. SERP COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS**
> Analyze what's currently ranking (even if you have to make educated inferences based on keyword type):
> - Common content formats: [Listicles / Long-form guides / Videos / Tools / Product pages]
> - Average word count of top 10 results: [Estimate range]
> - Content gaps: What are top-ranking pages missing that we could provide?
> - Differentiation angle: How can we make this better/different/more specific?
>
> **3. KEYWORD STRATEGY**
> - Primary keyword: [THE MAIN TARGET]
> - Secondary keywords (4-6): [Related terms to include naturally]
> - LSI keywords (6-10): [Semantic variations and related concepts Google expects to see]
> - Long-tail variations (3-5): [More specific versions of the main keyword]
> - Where to use primary keyword: [Title, H1, first 100 words, subheadings, meta description, URL slug]
>
> **4. CONTENT STRUCTURE**
> Provide a detailed outline:
>
> **Title Tag (60 characters max):**
> [Write 3 title options that include keyword + modifier/benefit]
>
> **Meta Description (155 characters max):**
> [Write 2 options that include keyword + value prop + CTA]
>
> **URL Slug:**
> [yoursite.com/suggested-slug]
>
> **H1 Headline:**
> [Primary keyword-focused headline — can be different from title tag]
>
> **Introduction (100-150 words):**
> - Hook that matches search intent
> - Include primary keyword in first 100 words naturally
> - Promise what the article will deliver
> - Optional: Add credibility statement
>
> **H2 Section Outline:**
> Break into 6-10 H2 sections:
>
> H2: [Section Headline — should include secondary keyword when natural]
> - Key points to cover: [Bullet list]
> - Supporting examples/data needed: [Specific type]
> - Approximate word count: [150-300 words]
> - Internal link opportunity: [Yes/No — if yes, suggest anchor text + target page]
>
> [Repeat for each section]
>
> **Conclusion (100-150 words):**
> - Recap main takeaway
> - Include CTA: [Subscribe / Download / Read related / Take action]
>
> **5. CONTENT REQUIREMENTS**
> - **Target word count**: [Based on competition + depth needed]
> - **Reading level**: [8th grade / 10th grade / Technical]
> - **Tone**: [Professional / Conversational / Academic / Friendly expert]
> - **Expertise signals to include**: [Author credentials / Data sources / Case studies / Original research]
> - **E-E-A-T signals**: [Experience examples / Expertise markers / Authority indicators / Trust elements]
>
> **6. VISUAL & MEDIA ELEMENTS**
> Suggest:
> - Featured image concept
> - 3-5 in-content visuals: [Screenshots / Diagrams / Charts / Examples / Infographics]
> - Video embed opportunity: [Yes/No — if yes, what type]
> - Interactive elements: [Calculator / Checklist / Quiz / Embed]
>
> **7. INTERNAL LINKING STRATEGY**
> - Target number of internal links: [3-7]
> - Suggested anchor text + target pages: [List 3-5 specific opportunities]
> - Contextual placement notes
>
> **8. EXTERNAL LINKING STRATEGY**
> - Number of external links: [2-5 to authoritative sources]
> - Types of sources to cite: [Research / Studies / Tools / Examples]
> - Link placement strategy: [Support claims / Provide additional resources]
>
> **9. OPTIMIZATION CHECKLIST**
> Before publishing, verify:
> - [ ] Primary keyword in: Title, H1, first 100 words, at least one H2, conclusion, meta description, URL
> - [ ] Readability score: [Target Flesch score]
> - [ ] Alt text on all images (include keyword in featured image alt text)
> - [ ] Schema markup: [Article / HowTo / FAQ / Review — which applies?]
> - [ ] Mobile-friendly formatting (short paragraphs, subheadings every 300 words)
> - [ ] Page speed optimization (compressed images, no render-blocking)
>
> **10. SUCCESS METRICS**
> Track these after publishing:
> - Ranking position for primary keyword (weeks 4, 8, 12)
> - Impressions and CTR in GSC
> - Bounce rate and time on page
> - Conversion rate for CTA
> - Backlinks acquired naturally
>
> Make this brief so detailed that anyone following it will create content with the best chance of ranking.

**When to use it:** Use this before writing any SEO-focused content when you want a data-driven brief that handles keyword research, content structure, and optimization requirements instead of guessing what Google wants.

**Pro tip:** After getting the brief, ask the AI to "analyze the top 3 ranking pages for [keyword] and tell me specifically what content angles they're missing that we should include" for an even stronger differentiation strategy.

## 8. Email Copy That Converts

**The Prompt:**

> You are a conversion copywriter who specializes in writing emails that get opened, read, and clicked.
>
> I need a high-converting email.
>
> **Email Details:**
> - Type: [Promotional / Launch / Nurture / Re-engagement / Cart abandonment / Onboarding / Newsletter with CTA]
> - Goal: [Get clicks to sales page / Book calls / Download resource / Reply to email / Purchase directly / Attend event]
> - Recipient: [Cold audience / Warm subscribers / Engaged segment / Past customers / Trial users]
> - Email position in sequence: [Standalone / Email #X in Y-email sequence]
>
> **Offer/Product Context:**
> - What you're promoting: [PRODUCT/SERVICE/RESOURCE]
> - Price point: [Free / $X / $X-$Y range / Premium $$$$]
> - Main benefit: [The #1 outcome/transformation]
> - Key objections: [List 2-3 reasons someone wouldn't buy/click]
> - Urgency/scarcity: [Deadline / Limited spots / Bonus expires / None]
>
> **Audience Context:**
> - What they already know about you: [First touch / Subscribed recently / Long-time subscriber / Past customer]
> - Their awareness level: [Unaware of problem / Problem-aware / Solution-aware / Product-aware / Most aware]
> - Primary pain point: [What frustration does this solve?]
>
> **Write a complete email with these components:**
>
> **1. SUBJECT LINE**
> Give me 5 subject line options using different psychological triggers:
>
> - **Curiosity**: [Create information gap]
> - **Benefit**: [Lead with outcome]
> - **Urgency**: [Time-sensitive angle]
> - **Personalization**: [Make it about them]
> - **Contrarian**: [Challenge expectation]
>
> For each, provide:
