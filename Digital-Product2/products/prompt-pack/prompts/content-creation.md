# Content Creation Prompts

## 1. Blog Post Architecture

**The Prompt:**

> You are a senior content strategist who has written 500+ viral blog posts. Write a complete blog post about [TOPIC].
>
> **Structure requirements:**
> - Opening hook: Start with a surprising statistic, counterintuitive claim, or scenario that creates a knowledge gap. No "In today's world" or generic introductions.
> - Subheadings: Each H2 should work as a standalone tweet—specific, bold, curiosity-triggering
> - Body: 1,500 words. Every 200-300 words, include a concrete example, case study, or data point. No theoretical fluff.
> - Conclusion: End with ONE specific action the reader can take in the next 10 minutes
>
> **Writing rules:**
> - Use active voice for 85%+ of sentences
> - Write at 8th-grade reading level (Flesch-Kincaid)
> - Zero filler phrases ("it's important to note", "as we all know", "in conclusion")
> - Make bold claims backed by specifics—this is the kind of post people bookmark
>
> Before you write, ask me: What's the reader's biggest misconception about this topic? Then write the post that shatters it.

**When to use it:** When you need a publication-ready blog post that actually gets read and shared, not just published.

**Pro tip:** Answer the AI's question about misconceptions—that one detail will make the entire post 10x more interesting than generic content.

## 2. Newsletter That People Actually Read

**The Prompt:**

> You are a newsletter writer with 50,000+ engaged subscribers. Write a newsletter issue about [TOPIC].
>
> **Format:**
> - Subject line: 6 words or less. Create curiosity or surprise, never describe the content directly.
> - Opening (2-3 sentences): Start with "You" or a provocative question. No preamble.
> - Main content (400-600 words): One core insight explained through a story, example, or case study. No listicles.
> - Closing: One sentence that makes the reader think differently + one specific action
> - P.S.: Add a counterintuitive observation or unexpected resource related to the topic
>
> **Voice:**
> - Write like you're texting a smart friend
> - Use short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
> - Include at least one "Wait, what?" moment that challenges conventional wisdom
> - End sentences with periods, not exclamation marks (you're confident, not desperate)
>
> First, ask me: What do most people get wrong about this topic? Then write the newsletter that corrects it with style.

**When to use it:** When you need a newsletter that gets opened, read completely, and forwarded to friends.

**Pro tip:** The P.S. is often the most-read part—use it to add a tactical resource or surprising fact that rewards people who read to the end.

## 3. Social Caption Batch Creator

**The Prompt:**

> You are a social media strategist who has generated 50M+ impressions. Create 10 social media captions about [TOPIC].
>
> **Requirements:**
> - Mix formats: 3 short (under 50 words), 4 medium (50-150 words), 3 long (150-300 words with line breaks)
> - Each caption must have a DIFFERENT opening pattern: question, bold claim, personal story, data point, contrarian take, scenario, quote reaction, before/after, misconception, prediction
> - Include clear formatting: Use line breaks, emojis strategically (2-3 max per caption), and bullet points for listy content
> - Every caption ends with either a question to drive comments OR a clear CTA
>
> **Voice:**
> - Authoritative but conversational—like you've actually done this 100 times
> - Zero cringe: No "Let's dive in", "Here's the tea", "Buckle up", or "Mind. Blown."
> - Lead with value in the first sentence (people decide to read in 1.5 seconds)
>
> First, tell me: What platform? (LinkedIn/Twitter/Instagram) Then I'll optimize each caption for that algorithm and audience behavior.

**When to use it:** When you need two weeks of social content written in 5 minutes instead of grinding for 2 hours.

**Pro tip:** Paste these into your scheduling tool as-is, but change 2-3 words in each to match your actual voice before posting.

## 4. Video Script Framework

**The Prompt:**

> You are a video script writer whose content has generated 100M+ views. Write a video script about [TOPIC].
>
> **Structure (for 3-8 minute video):**
> - HOOK (first 8 seconds): Start with a pattern interrupt—bold claim, shocking stat, visual contrast, or unanswered question. Write the EXACT words to say.
> - RETENTION LOOP (seconds 9-15): Promise a specific outcome by the end + tease the "but here's what no one tells you" moment
> - MAIN CONTENT (80% of video): Break into 3-5 sections with mini-cliffhangers between each. Use "But here's the problem...", "This is where it gets interesting...", "And this is the part that changed everything..."
> - PAYOFF (final 30 seconds): Deliver the promised insight + one action they can take today
> - CTA: Tell them exactly what to do next (like, subscribe, comment with X, click link)
>
> **Script formatting:**
> - Write [VISUAL DIRECTION] in brackets for every major point
> - Mark **emphasis words** for delivery
> - Include (pause) where needed
> - Add {B-ROLL SUGGESTION} for supplementary footage
>
> Before I write, ask me: What platform? (YouTube/TikTok/Instagram Reels) Then I'll adjust pacing and style accordingly.

**When to use it:** When you need a video script that keeps viewers watching instead of scrolling away in 3 seconds.

**Pro tip:** Read the script out loud before filming—if you stumble over a sentence, rewrite it to match how you actually talk.

## 5. Headline Swipe File Generator

**The Prompt:**

> You are a direct response copywriter who has written 10,000+ headlines. Generate 25 headline variations for [TOPIC/OFFER].
>
> **Formula mix (use all of these patterns):**
> - Numbered list: "7 [Thing] That [Outcome]"
> - How-to: "How to [Desired Outcome] Without [Common Obstacle]"
> - Question: "What If [Surprising Scenario]?"
> - Negative angle: "Stop [Doing X] Until You [Know Y]"
> - Timeframe: "The [Timeframe] [Thing] to [Outcome]"
> - Contrarian: "[Common Belief] Is Wrong. Here's Why."
> - Before/After: "From [Bad State] to [Good State] in [Timeframe]"
> - Secret/Reveal: "The [Adjective] [Thing] [Authority Figure] Doesn't Want You to Know"
> - Mistake: "Are You Making These [Number] [Topic] Mistakes?"
> - Ultimate guide: "The Only [Topic] Guide You'll Ever Need"
>
> **Rules:**
> - Keep 80% under 60 characters (social media optimized)
> - Use specific numbers, not ranges ("7" not "7-10")
> - Include power words: proven, secret, fast, simple, guaranteed, free, new, easy, amazing
> - Avoid: "that", "things", "stuff", "tips", "tricks" (be more specific)
>
> First, ask me: Is this for a blog post, product, video, or lead magnet? Then I'll optimize the headline psychology accordingly.

**When to use it:** When you're staring at a blank page trying to write ONE good headline—get 25 instead and pick the best.

**Pro tip:** Test your top 3 headlines by asking 3 people "Which would you click?"—your favorite is often NOT the winner.

## 6. Content Repurposing Machine

**The Prompt:**

> You are a content strategist who extracts 10 pieces of content from one source. I'll give you one piece of content, and you'll repurpose it into multiple formats.
>
> **Paste your source content below, then I'll create:**
> 1. **Twitter/X thread** (8-12 tweets with hooks and transitions)
> 2. **LinkedIn post** (200-300 words, professional tone)
> 3. **Instagram carousel** (8-10 slide outlines with headlines and key points)
> 4. **Email newsletter** (400-600 words)
> 5. **YouTube video outline** (intro hook, 3-5 main points, outro CTA)
> 6. **5 short-form video scripts** (under 60 seconds each, TikTok/Reels style)
> 7. **Blog post outline** (H2 sections with subpoints)
> 8. **3 quote graphics** (pull the most powerful statements)
> 9. **Pinterest pin titles** (5 variations, keyword-optimized)
> 10. **Podcast talking points** (conversational outline with stories)
>
> **My process:**
> - Extract the core insight
> - Adapt the hook for each platform's algorithm and audience behavior
> - Reformat structure to match consumption patterns (short-form vs. long-form)
> - Keep the unique perspective but change the delivery
>
> Paste your source content now, and tell me: What format is it currently? (blog, video, podcast, etc.)

**When to use it:** When you spent 2 hours creating one piece of content and want to get 30+ social posts from it without starting over.

**Pro tip:** Repurpose your best-performing content from 6-12 months ago—your audience has likely grown and most people never saw it.

## 7. SEO Content Brief Builder

**The Prompt:**

> You are an SEO content strategist who has ranked 500+ articles on page one. Create a complete content brief for [TOPIC/KEYWORD].
>
> **Brief includes:**
> 1. **Primary keyword**: [Auto-detected or I'll ask]
> 2. **Search intent**: What the searcher actually wants to accomplish
> 3. **Headline options** (3 variations under 60 characters, keyword in first half)
> 4. **Meta description** (155 characters, includes keyword + benefit)
> 5. **Target word count**: [Based on competitor analysis I'll do]
> 6. **Required H2 sections** (6-8 subheadings that match search intent + related searches)
> 7. **Semantic keywords to include naturally** (15-20 LSI terms)
> 8. **Questions to answer** (from "People Also Ask")
> 9. **Content angle**: The unique POV that differentiates this from the 10 other articles on page one
> 10. **Internal linking opportunities** (suggest 3-5 relevant topics to link to)
>
> **What makes this brief work:**
> - It's not just keyword stuffing—it's answering the actual search intent
> - The structure matches what already ranks (but with better execution)
> - Built-in differentiation so you're not creating the 11th clone article
>
> First, tell me: Just the topic or keyword. I'll research the search landscape and build the complete brief.

**When to use it:** When you want to rank on Google without guessing what to write or how to structure it.

**Pro tip:** Write the article in the exact H2 order the brief gives you—that structure is based on what Google already rewards.

## 8. Email Copy That Converts

**The Prompt:**

> You are a conversion copywriter who has written emails that generate 7 figures in revenue. Write a sales email about [PRODUCT/OFFER].
>
> **Email structure:**
> - **Subject line**: 6 words or less. Use curiosity, urgency, or direct benefit. Test 3 variations.
> - **Preview text**: 40 characters that extends the subject line's hook
> - **Opening** (2-3 sentences): Start with "You" or a relatable pain point. No pleasantries.
> - **The problem** (100-150 words): Agitate the specific pain your offer solves. Make them feel it.
> - **The solution** (150-200 words): Introduce your offer as the bridge between pain and desired outcome
> - **Social proof** (50-100 words): One specific customer result, not vague testimonials
> - **The offer** (50-75 words): What they get, what it costs, why now
> - **CTA** (one sentence): Tell them exactly what to do. Use first-person ("Yes, I want [specific benefit]")
> - **P.S.**: Restate the main benefit or add urgency/scarcity element
>
> **Writing rules:**
> - Use short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
> - Include one power word per paragraph (proven, guaranteed, fast, simple, exclusive, limited)
> - Address objections before they arise
> - Zero hype—be confident, not desperate
>
> First, ask me: What's the #1 objection people have before buying? Then I'll write an email that dissolves it.

**When to use it:** When you need an email that actually drives sales, not just opens and deletes.

**Pro tip:** Send this email at 10 AM on Tuesday or Wednesday—highest open and click-through rates for most audiences.

## 9. Landing Page Copy Blueprint

**The Prompt:**

> You are a landing page copywriter who converts at 15%+ (top 1% of the industry). Write complete landing page copy for [PRODUCT/OFFER].
>
> **Landing page sections:**
>
> **HERO (above the fold):**
> - Headline: One sentence. Outcome-focused, not feature-focused. Under 10 words.
> - Subheadline: One sentence. Explain how or add credibility. Under 15 words.
> - CTA button: 2-4 words, first-person language ("Get My [Benefit]")
>
> **PROBLEM (section 2):**
> - 3 pain points written as "You've tried [common solution] but [why it fails]..."
> - Agitate the frustration without being manipulative
>
> **SOLUTION (section 3):**
> - Introduce your offer as the bridge
> - 3 key benefits (outcomes, not features)
> - One sentence each, starting with the benefit
>
> **HOW IT WORKS (section 4):**
> - 3-step process
> - Each step: Number + Action + Result
> - Make it sound simple and fast
>
> **SOCIAL PROOF (section 5):**
> - 2-3 specific customer results with numbers
> - Format: "[Name] got [specific result] in [timeframe]"
>
> **OFFER (section 6):**
> - What's included (bulleted list, benefit-focused)
> - Price + any guarantee
> - Urgency element (limited spots, bonus expires, etc.)
>
> **FAQ (section 7):**
> - 5 questions that address the biggest objections
> - Keep answers short (2-3 sentences)
>
> **FINAL CTA (section 8):**
> - Restate the main benefit
> - Repeat the CTA button
> - Add a risk-reversal statement under the button
>
> First, ask me: What's the main result people want from this offer? Then I'll write copy that makes that outcome irresistible.

**When to use it:** When you need a landing page that converts cold traffic into buyers, not just collects emails that go nowhere.

**Pro tip:** Put your pricing above the fold if your offer is under $100, below the fold (after social proof) if it's over $100.

## 10. Storytelling Framework Activator

**The Prompt:**

> You are a master storyteller who has written 1,000+ viral stories. Take my [TOPIC/MESSAGE] and turn it into a compelling narrative.
>
> **Story structure (choose the best fit):**
>
> **Option A: The Hero's Journey (Transformation story)**
> - Ordinary world: Where I/they started
> - Call to adventure: The moment everything changed
> - Challenges: 3 specific obstacles faced
> - Breakthrough: The turning point
> - Resolution: Where I/they are now
> - Lesson: What this means for the reader
>
> **Option B: The Mistake Story (Credibility builder)**
> - The setup: What I thought would work
> - The mistake: What I did wrong (be specific)
> - The consequence: What it cost me
> - The lesson: What I learned
> - The application: How the reader can avoid this
>
> **Option C: The Underdog Story (Engagement magnet)**
> - The disadvantage: What was stacked against me/them
> - The determination: Why quitting wasn't an option
> - The strategy: The unconventional approach taken
> - The victory: The specific win
> - The insight: What made the difference
>
> **Writing rules:**
> - Use sensory details (what I saw, heard, felt)
> - Include dialogue when possible (even internal dialogue)
> - Show vulnerability—the messier parts make it relatable
> - End with a universal truth, not a moral lecture
> - Keep it under 600 words
>
> Tell me: What's the core message or lesson you want to convey? Then I'll ask 2-3 questions to pull out the story details and write the full narrative.

**When to use it:** When you have a point to make but need to wrap it in a story that people actually remember and share.

**Pro tip:** The best stories include a specific moment of failure or doubt—that's what makes the success feel real instead of humble-bragging.

## 11. Content Calendar Architect

**The Prompt:**

> You are a content strategist who plans 90 days of content in 30 minutes. Create a complete content calendar for [TOPIC/BUSINESS].
>
> **Calendar deliverables:**
> 1. **30 content ideas** (mix of formats: blog posts, social posts, videos, emails, lead magnets)
> 2. **Content themes**: Organized into 4-5 pillar topics that repeat monthly
> 3. **Publishing frequency**: Optimized schedule based on platform and audience
> 4. **Content mix**: 60% educational, 30% engagement, 10% promotional (adjust if needed)
> 5. **Repurposing plan**: Show which long-form pieces become multiple short-form assets
>
> **Each content idea includes:**
> - Working title
> - Format (blog, video, carousel, thread, etc.)
> - Core angle (the unique perspective)
> - Primary CTA (what action do you want them to take?)
> - Difficulty (easy/medium/hard to create)
>
> **Strategic elements:**
> - Ideas build on each other (not random topics)
> - Mix of evergreen and timely content
> - Balance between beginner and advanced
> - Include content gaps competitors aren't covering
>
> First, tell me: What's your main business goal right now? (grow email list, launch product, build authority, drive sales) Then I'll ask 2-3 clarifying questions and build the full 30-day calendar.

**When to use it:** When you're tired of posting randomly and want a strategic content plan that actually builds toward a business goal.

**Pro tip:** Batch-create all your "easy" content first—that builds momentum and makes the harder pieces feel less overwhelming.

## 12. Authority Article Outliner

**The Prompt:**

> You are an investigative journalist who writes the definitive article on complex topics. Create a comprehensive outline for the ultimate guide to [TOPIC].
>
> **Article requirements:**
> - 3,000-5,000 words (this is the ONE article people bookmark and reference for years)
> - 15-20 H2 sections minimum
> - Each section has 3-5 H3 subpoints
> - This should be THE resource—comprehensive but not overwhelming
>
> **Outline structure:**
>
> **I. INTRODUCTION**
> - Hook: Counterintuitive claim or surprising stat
> - Why this matters: The stakes
> - What this guide covers: Content preview
> - What makes this different: Your unique angle
>
> **II-IV. FOUNDATIONAL SECTIONS** (3 sections)
> - Cover the basics, definitions, common misconceptions
> - Include history or context if relevant
> - Address why most people get this wrong
>
> **V-XII. CORE CONTENT SECTIONS** (8 sections)
> - The deep expertise—strategies, frameworks, case studies
> - Each section includes: concept explanation, real example, common mistakes, implementation steps
> - Build from beginner to advanced progressively
>
> **XIII. COMPARISON/ALTERNATIVES**
> - Compare different approaches or tools
> - Pros/cons for each
> - Recommendations based on scenarios
>
> **XIV. IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP**
> - Step-by-step action plan
> - What to do first, second, third
> - Timeline expectations
>
> **XV. ADVANCED TACTICS**
> - Expert-level strategies
> - Edge cases and nuances
> - What to do once you've mastered the basics
>
> **XVI. COMMON PITFALLS**
> - Mistakes to avoid
> - Red flags to watch for
> - How to course-correct
>
> **XVII. TOOLS & RESOURCES**
> - Recommended tools (with brief descriptions)
> - Further reading
> - Communities or experts to follow
>
> **XVIII. FUTURE TRENDS**
> - Where this is heading
> - What to prepare for
> - Predictions
>
> **XIX. CONCLUSION**
> - Key takeaways (3-5 bullets)
> - One next action
> - Reminder of why this matters
>
> **XX. FAQ**
> - 10-15 questions addressing every remaining objection or confusion
>
> First, tell me: What's the topic? Then I'll ask 2-3 questions about your expertise level and audience, and deliver the complete outline with H2 and H3 sections ready to write from.

**When to use it:** When you want to write THE definitive article that ranks #1, gets linked to constantly, and establishes you as the authority.

**Pro tip:** Don't try to write this in one sitting—tackle 2-3 sections per day and you'll finish a comprehensive guide in a week without burnout.
