# Productivity Prompts

## 1. Daily Battle Plan

**The Prompt:**

> You are my strategic operations officer. Here's everything competing for my attention today:
>
> [Paste your to-do list, calendar items, messages you need to handle — dump it all]
>
> Give me:
> 1. **The ONE thing** — What I must finish today no matter what happens
> 2. **The brutal cut** — 3 things to delete, delegate, or defer with zero guilt
> 3. **Time-blocked schedule** — Hour-by-hour plan from now until end of day
> 4. **Failure protocol** — If I only get 4 hours of work done, which 4 hours?
>
> Be ruthlessly specific. Use actual times (not "morning"). If you need to know my energy peaks or constraints, ask me one follow-up question.

**When to use it:** Every morning or when you feel overwhelmed by competing priorities.

**Pro tip:** Paste this prompt result into a note you keep visible all day — checking it prevents decision fatigue and reactive mode.

## 2. Goal Setting That Sticks

**The Prompt:**

> Act as a behavioral design expert who specializes in making goals actually happen.
>
> Here's what I want to achieve: [Describe your goal in one sentence]
>
> Design a goal structure that includes:
> 1. **Minimum viable version** — The smallest win I can hit in 7 days that proves progress
> 2. **The daily proof** — One specific action I do every day (15 minutes max) that compounds
> 3. **The forcing function** — External accountability mechanism with real consequences
> 4. **Failure tells** — 3 early warning signs I'm drifting off track
> 5. **The restart protocol** — Exactly what I do when I miss 2+ days
>
> No motivational fluff. Just tactical clarity.

**When to use it:** When setting quarterly goals, New Year intentions, or any target that matters enough to design properly.

**Pro tip:** Screenshot the forcing function and failure tells — those two sections prevent 90% of goal abandonment.

## 3. Decision Clarity Engine

**The Prompt:**

> You are a decision strategist who cuts through analysis paralysis.
>
> Here's the decision I'm stuck on:
> [Describe the choice you're facing — include what you're weighing between]
>
> Analyze this using:
> 1. **10/10/10 lens** — How will I feel about this in 10 days, 10 months, 10 years?
> 2. **Regret minimization** — Which choice will I regret less at age 80?
> 3. **Hell yes or no** — Does either option make me feel "hell yes"? If not, why?
> 4. **Second-order effects** — What doors does each choice open or close?
> 5. **The call** — Tell me which to choose and the exact first step to take tomorrow morning.
>
> Be opinionated. I need a clear recommendation, not balanced pros/cons.

**When to use it:** When you've been circling a decision for more than 3 days without pulling the trigger.

**Pro tip:** The 10/10/10 lens alone solves 70% of decisions — use just that section if you're short on time.

## 4. Eisenhower Priority Matrix

**The Prompt:**

> You are a time management strategist. Here's what's on my plate:
>
> [Paste your tasks, projects, requests — everything you're juggling]
>
> Sort these into the Eisenhower Matrix and give me:
> 1. **DO NOW** (urgent + important) — With time estimates
> 2. **SCHEDULE** (important, not urgent) — Specific calendar blocks to assign them
> 3. **DELEGATE** (urgent, not important) — Who should handle each + handoff message template
> 4. **DELETE** (neither urgent nor important) — Permission to drop these entirely
>
> Be honest about what's fake-urgent. If something feels urgent but isn't important, call it out.

**When to use it:** Weekly planning sessions or when you feel busy but not productive.

**Pro tip:** The DELETE section is the most valuable — read it first and feel the weight lift before organizing what remains.

## 5. Meeting Agenda That Respects Time

**The Prompt:**

> You are an executive meeting designer. I need an agenda for this meeting:
>
> **Purpose:** [One sentence — what outcome you need]
> **Attendees:** [Who's joining]
> **Length:** [How long you have]
>
> Build me an agenda with:
> 1. **Pre-meeting homework** — What attendees review beforehand (5 min max)
> 2. **Time-boxed sections** — Minute-by-minute blocks with what we're deciding or creating
> 3. **Decision framework** — How we'll actually choose/agree (vote, consensus, single decider)
> 4. **Output format** — Exactly what artifact we produce (doc, decision, action list)
> 5. **Success statement** — One sentence I can say at the end to confirm we hit the goal
>
> No "discuss" or "brainstorm" — only sections that produce something concrete.

**When to use it:** Before scheduling any meeting longer than 15 minutes.

**Pro tip:** Send the pre-meeting homework and decision framework to attendees 24 hours early — cuts meeting time by 40%.

## 6. Weekly Review System

**The Prompt:**

> You are my executive coach running our weekly review session. Here's my data:
>
> [Paste: completed tasks, meetings attended, wins, fires you fought, what felt hard]
>
> Run the review:
> 1. **Pattern recognition** — What 2-3 patterns do you see in how I spent my time?
> 2. **Energy audit** — Which activities drained me? Which energized me?
> 3. **The silent killer** — What important-not-urgent thing did I avoid all week?
> 4. **Next week's edge** — One specific improvement to how I work (not what I work on)
> 5. **Win tracking** — Frame my biggest accomplishment in a way that feels significant
>
> Be direct about where I'm drifting off course. I need coaching, not cheerleading.

**When to use it:** Every Friday afternoon or Sunday evening to close the loop on the week.

**Pro tip:** Run this 4 weeks in a row and the pattern recognition section will reveal your actual work style vs. your imagined one.

## 7. Time Audit Analyzer

**The Prompt:**

> You are a time efficiency analyst. Here's how I actually spent my time recently:
>
> [Paste: calendar from last week, rough time log, or just describe a typical day in detail]
>
> Analyze and report:
> 1. **The expensive hours** — Time spent on low-value work that cost me high-value opportunities
> 2. **Hidden time tax** — Recurring small tasks that add up (emails, context switching, etc.)
> 3. **Batching opportunities** — Which tasks I should group together to save 5+ hours/week
> 4. **The $1000/hour question** — If I could only work 4 hours this week, which 4 hours create the most value?
> 5. **Deletion candidates** — 3 things I do regularly that I should stop doing entirely
>
> Give me dollar values where possible (if I bill at $X/hour, this wastes $Y/week).

**When to use it:** Quarterly, or whenever you feel busy but your revenue/results aren't growing.

**Pro tip:** The hidden time tax section reveals death by a thousand cuts — delegate or automate those first.

## 8. Habit Design Lab

**The Prompt:**

> Act as a habit formation specialist. I want to build this habit:
>
> [Describe the habit you want — be specific about frequency and what "doing it" looks like]
>
> Design the system:
> 1. **Minimum viable habit** — The smallest version I can do even on terrible days (2 min max)
> 2. **Trigger design** — Specific existing behavior I anchor this to (not "every morning")
> 3. **Friction removal** — 3 obstacles to remove before I start (prep, environment, etc.)
> 4. **Tracking mechanism** — Dead simple way to track without an app (visual, physical)
> 5. **The 2-day rule** — Never skip twice. If I miss one day, what's the forcing function for day 2?
>
> Assume I have zero discipline. Design for laziness.

**When to use it:** When starting any new habit you want to maintain for 90+ days.

**Pro tip:** The trigger design is everything — "after I pour coffee" beats "in the morning" 10 out of 10 times.

## 9. Project Breakdown Engine

**The Prompt:**

> You are a project architect who turns vague ideas into executable plans. Here's my project:
>
> [Describe what you're trying to build/ship/complete]
>
> Break it down:
> 1. **Minimum shippable version** — What's the smallest thing I can finish in 2 weeks that works?
> 2. **Sequential phases** — 4-5 phases from MVP to full vision (each deliverable/testable)
> 3. **First 3 moves** — Extremely specific next actions I can start today (30 min each)
> 4. **Stall points** — Where will I probably get stuck? What's the plan for each stall?
> 5. **Done looks like** — Concrete description of what "finished" means for each phase
>
> No theory. Only moves I can execute.

**When to use it:** At the start of any project larger than a day's work.

**Pro tip:** Start phase 2 planning only after shipping phase 1 — premature planning kills momentum.

## 10. Delegation Framework

**The Prompt:**

> You are a delegation strategist. Here's what's overwhelming me:
>
> [List tasks, responsibilities, recurring work that's on your plate]
>
> Help me delegate:
> 1. **Delegation candidates** — Which 5 items should I delegate first (ranked by ROI)
> 2. **Role definition** — For the top 3, define the role: title, responsibilities, success metrics
> 3. **Handoff package** — What documentation/training each needs (keep it minimal)
> 4. **Trust protocol** — How I check quality without micromanaging (frequency, format)
> 5. **Failure recovery** — If they drop the ball, what's my 15-minute fix?
>
> Assume I'm scared to let go. Convince me why each delegation is safe.

**When to use it:** When you're working 50+ hour weeks but revenue isn't growing proportionally.

**Pro tip:** Delegate the $25/hour tasks first (even if you do them well) — frees time for $500/hour work.

## 11. Energy Management Optimizer

**The Prompt:**

> You are an energy optimization consultant. Here's my reality:
>
> [Describe: your energy peaks/crashes, what drains you, what energizes you, current sleep/exercise/diet baseline]
>
> Optimize my energy:
> 1. **Energy map** — Ideal schedule that matches high-energy work to high-energy hours
> 2. **Protection blocks** — Which meetings/tasks to ban during peak hours (be specific)
> 3. **Recovery rituals** — 3 micro-resets I can do in 5 minutes when energy crashes
> 4. **The Sunday reset** — Weekend routine that sets me up for a strong Monday
> 5. **Energy ROI** — One lifestyle change that gives me 20% more useful hours per week
>
> Be specific about timing (not "morning" but "8:30-11:00 AM").

**When to use it:** When you have time available but feel too drained to use it effectively.

**Pro tip:** The protection blocks have 10x ROI — defend your peak 3 hours like your business depends on it (it does).

## 12. Focus Session Designer

**The Prompt:**

> You are a deep work architect. I need to make serious progress on:
>
> [Describe the work that requires deep focus]
>
> Design my focus session:
> 1. **Session length** — Optimal block based on this work type (don't assume 2 hours)
> 2. **Pre-session ritual** — 5-minute routine that gets me into state (specific actions)
> 3. **Environment design** — What to close, block, silence, and what to have visible
> 4. **Progress milestones** — Checkpoints every 25-30 min so I know I'm on track
> 5. **Exit protocol** — How to end the session and capture momentum for next time
>
> Assume I'll be tempted to check messages. Design against my weaknesses.

**When to use it:** Before any work session where interruption would cost you hours of recovery time.

**Pro tip:** The exit protocol is underrated — spend 2 minutes writing "next step" so you don't lose 20 minutes reorienting tomorrow.
